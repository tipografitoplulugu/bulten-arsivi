## Martin Majoor founded his own type foundry.

*Image: © [Martin Majoor](https://twitter.com/martinmajoor/status/1409879269111742481)*

Dutch type designer [Martin Majoor](http://www.martinmajoor.com), who designed popular typefaces such as [Scala](https://www.fontshop.com/families/ff-scala), [Seria](https://www.fontshop.com/families/ff-seria), [Nexus](https://www.fontshop.com/superfamilies/ff-nexus), and [Questa](https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/questa), [announced](https://twitter.com/martinmajoor/status/1409879269111742481) that he has established a type foundry bearing his own name.

## A typography book from Dan Cederholm

*Image: © [simplebits.com](https://simplebits.com/products/twenty-bits-fonts)*

Dribbble co-founder [Dan Cederholm](https://simplebits.com/pages/about) published a [book](https://simplebits.com/products/twenty-bits-fonts) on type design. The book, which does not contain exhaustive information on the mechanics of letterforms and typographic theory, explores twenty recommendations drawn from the author’s personal experiences.

## Mathematical typefaces from a father-and-son team

*Image: © [Is This Some Kind of Code?](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/06/25/science/puzzles-fonts-math-demaine.html)*

Scientists use typefaces every day to express their research in writing. What if the typeface itself conveyed the spirit of the research? Or what if not just the text itself, but the way it is written drew the reader into science? Setting out from such questions, the father-and-son team Erik and Martin Demaine, who describe themselves as *algorithmic typographers*, have [designed a series of typefaces](https://erikdemaine.org/fonts/) inspired by mathematics. [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/06/25/science/puzzles-fonts-math-demaine.html) also interviewed the team, who published their work in an article titled [Fun with Fonts: Algorithmic Typography](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/Fonts_TCS/paper.pdf). 🇬🇧

## Christopher Slye became the new president of Type Network.

*Image: © [TypeNetwork](https://www.typenetwork.com/news/article/christopher-slye-type-network)*

> “There is great interest in custom typefaces for companies and products.” Christopher Slye

[Christopher Slye](https://www.typenetwork.com/news/article/christopher-slye-type-network) has become the new president of [Type Network](https://www.typenetwork.com), founded in 2016 to bring together independent type foundries and designers to offer a curated type collection. Taking over the role from [Paley Dreier](http://paley.nyc), Slye was previously the manager of all type-related activities at Adobe, including Adobe Fonts, for over twenty years. Also a type designer himself, Slye designed his first typeface, [Elmhurst](https://store.typenetwork.com/foundry/fontbureau/fonts/?name=Elmhurst) — still available on Type Network — in 1997.

## An article on legibility from Ralf Herrmann

*Image: © [Typography.guru](https://typography.guru/journal/how-do-we-read-words-and-how-should-we-set-them-r19/)*

> “Word shape is no longer a viable model of word recognition. The majority of scientific evidence shows that we recognise the letters that make up the word and then use this visual information to recognise the word.”

> Kevin Larson

[Ralf Herrmann](https://twitter.com/opentype) has written [an article](https://typography.guru/journal/how-do-we-read-words-and-how-should-we-set-them-r19/) addressing important information on the concept of legibility, discussing how science has overturned conventional ideas that we perceive words as a whole and how ascenders and descenders in typographic anatomy support efficient reading. Herrmann’s article is significant in reminding us that, especially regarding legibility and readability, rather than relying on memorized assumptions without considering ambient environments and conditions, one must consult the findings of scientific research conducted in this field! 🇬🇧

## A new publishing platform for type: Fontstand News

*Image: © [A scene from the Fontstand Conference in Zagreb, Croatia, where more than thirty Fontstand co-founders gathered.](https://fontstand.com/news/essays/introducing-fontstand-news/)*

Typeface testing and rental app [Fontstand](https://fontstand.com) is launching a new publishing platform called [Fontstand News](https://fontstand.com/news/essays/introducing-fontstand-news/) to encourage in-depth discussion about type. Pointing out that in an era where there are more educational opportunities and more type is designed than at any time in history, it is surprising to encounter less discussion about type than ever, the platform is spearheaded by [Peter Biľak](https://peterbilak.com) and [Andrej Krátky](https://www.typotheque.com/authors/andrej_kratky) and edited by type and design historian [Dan Reynolds](https://www.typeoff.de/about-dan-reynolds/), known from [TypeOff](https://www.typeoff.de), who focuses on late 19th-century type foundries.

The [first book review](https://fontstand.com/news/book-reviews/a-grammar-of-typography/) on Fontstand News is Mark Argetsinger’s book [A Grammar of Typography: Classical Book Design in the Digital Age](https://www.amazon.com/Grammar-Typography-Classical-Design-Digital/dp/1567926533).

## Hieroglyphic printing press revived in Cairo.

*Image: Type composed of hieroglyphs in the former IFAO printing house in 1990. [© Sciences et Avenir](https://www.sciencesetavenir.fr/archeo-paleo/archeologie/le-second-souffle-d-une-imprimerie-a-hieroglyphes-au-caire_155695)*

The printing press of the [French Institute of Oriental Archaeology](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/) (IFAO) in Cairo, established in the early 20th century and housing type composed of hieroglyphs, has been [brought back to life](https://www.sciencesetavenir.fr/archeo-paleo/archeologie/le-second-souffle-d-une-imprimerie-a-hieroglyphes-au-caire_155695). 🇫🇷

## Helvetica Now Variable

*Image: © [Monotype](https://twitter.com/Monotype/status/1413656250840227843)*

Monotype announced the variable version of Helvetica Now [via its Twitter account](https://twitter.com/Monotype/status/1413656250840227843). With the goal of creating a Helvetica that meets the needs of today's designers and creatives, the update process—involving dozens of designers and engineers—began in December 2014, and following five years of work, [Helvetica Now](https://www.monotype.com/fonts/helvetica-now) was released in 2019. Offering over one million weights in a single font file and over two and a half million in the italic version, the new [Helvetica Now Variable](https://www.monotype.com/fonts/helvetica-now-variable) delivers customized options across the weight, optical size, and width axes.

## Reynold Ruffins passed away.

*Image: Reynold Ruffins (1930–2021)*

Painter, illustrator, and graphic designer [Reynold Ruffins](http://www.reynoldruffins.com), born in the United States in 1930, passed away on July 11, 2021. Ruffins, who co-founded [Push Pin Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push_Pin_Studios) in 1954 alongside Milton Glaser, Edward Sorel, and Seymour Chwast, was renowned for his stylistic versatility.

## Wolfgang Weingart passed away.

*Image: Wolfgang Weingart (1941–2021)*

Following Reynold Ruffins, another sad piece of news came from Switzerland. Internationally renowned graphic designer and typographer [Wolfgang Weingart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Weingart) passed away on July 12, 2021, in Basel, Switzerland. Born in 1941 in the Salem Valley of Germany, near the Swiss border, Weingart defied the traditional rules of the Swiss Style in typography, pioneering the *New Wave* or *Swiss Punk* styles.

## First digital typeface designed for Welsh

*Image: The eight digraphs of the Welsh language in serif (top) and sans serif (bottom). © [deezen](https://www.dezeen.com/2020/03/18/welsh-language-typeface-font-smorgasbord/?mc_cid=ad24b24ba3&mc_eid=48a95a3cf1)*

Cardiff and Amsterdam-based design studio [Smörgåsbord](https://www.smorgasbordstudio.com/) is designing a typeface that allows the unique characteristics of the Welsh language to be expressed. Developed in collaboration with [Colophon Type Foundry](https://www.colophon-foundry.org/), the typeface named *Cymru Wales* includes the eight digraphs that distinguish the Welsh alphabet from the English alphabet. The project was commissioned by the Welsh government as part of the nation's rebranding.
