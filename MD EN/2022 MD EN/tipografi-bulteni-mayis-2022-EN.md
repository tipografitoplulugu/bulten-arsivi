## “Do We Remember the Visual Appearance of Words?”

*Image: © [Design Regression](https://designregression.com/research/the-sequel-to-exploring-disfluency-do-we-remember-the-visual-appearance-of-words)*

Mary Dyson and David Březina [investigated](https://designregression.com/research/the-sequel-to-exploring-disfluency-do-we-remember-the-visual-appearance-of-words) whether memory judgments affect designers’ heightened sensitivity to typographic representation for items typeset in a less legible typeface such as [Sans Forgetica](https://sansforgetica.rmit.edu.au). The study found that designers' memory judgments were more affected by the legibility of items compared to non-designers. 🇬🇧

## The effect of font choice on reading speed

*Image: © [nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/best-font-for-online-reading/)*

Which font or fonts are best for online reading? Unfortunately, there is no single, definitive answer. In the [results of a study](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/best-font-for-online-reading/) conducted with 352 participants by Shaun Wallace and his team from Adobe, a 35% difference in online reading speeds was found between fonts with the best and worst legibility. As people get older, they read 11% slower every 20 years. 🇬🇧

## Nebiolo’s typefaces

*Image: © [Fontstand](https://fontstand.com/news/essays/typefaces-in-search-of-an-author/)*

As part of the [Nebiolo History Project](http://nebiolohistory.org), Riccardo Olocco published [an article](https://fontstand.com/news/essays/typefaces-in-search-of-an-author/) featuring an analysis of the archival materials that survived after the closure of Nebiolo, one of Italy’s most famous type foundries. 🇬🇧 The resulting data aims to define the role played by the type design office known as the *Studio Artistico* in creating the Nebiolo typefaces it produced. Until its closure in 1978, the Turin-based Nebiolo company was Italy's major type foundry both nationally and internationally for most of the twentieth century. Founded in 1852, Nebiolo successfully expanded as an important printing press manufacturer. It dominated the Italian market in the production of printing machinery and foundry type until the end of letterpress printing in the 1970s.

## What happens if you let a tree design type?

*Image: © Bjørn Karmann*

[Bjørn Karmann](https://bjoernkarmann.dk) turned the letters he carved into the bark of a tree in 2015 and documented each year into a type design called [Occlusion Grotesque](https://bjoernkarmann.dk/occlusion-grotesque). The project explores what it means to design with nature and under nature’s conditions. 🇬🇧

## The history of Javanese script and its relationship with contemporary design

*Image: © [Typegeist](https://typegeist.org/article/rediscovering-design-in-a-supplanted-script-the-case-of-javanese)*

When Indonesia gained independence in 1945, the Latin alphabet was made mandatory for the country's administrative affairs, and the everyday use of the Javanese script effectively ceased. Although the Javanese script is still taught as a mandatory subject in schools across various parts of [Java](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cava), most people feel alienated from the language and view it as a static relic rather than a living alphabet. In his [article](https://typegeist.org/article/rediscovering-design-in-a-supplanted-script-the-case-of-javanese), Aditya Bayu Perdana discusses the turbulent history of the Javanese script and its relationship with contemporary design. 🇬🇧

## The Gutenberg Prize has found its recipients.

*Image: © [Prof. Dr. Jeffrey F. Hamburger and Prof. Eckehart Schumacher-Gebler](https://twitter.com/GutenbergInt/status/1521791753321385984)*

The winners of the [Gutenberg Prize](https://www.gutenberg-gesellschaft.de/en/gutenberg-prize/), awarded since 1968 by the [International Gutenberg Society](https://www.gutenberg-gesellschaft.de), have been announced. For the first time, the 2022 prize was awarded to two individuals. [Prof. Dr. Jeffrey F. Hamburger](https://haa.fas.harvard.edu/people/jeffrey-f-hamburger), an expert in late medieval book illustration and the transition to the era of the printing press, shared the prize with printing professor [Eckehart Schumacher-Gebler](https://www.hbksaar.de/personen/details/schumachergebler). It was stated that one of the reasons for the jury’s decision to divide the prize this year was that both laureates achieved equally outstanding accomplishments in their relation to Johannes Gutenberg’s legacy: both the scholarly investigation of Gutenberg’s heritage and the continuation of the letterpress tradition. 🇬🇧

## A new book from TypeTogether

*Image: © [TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com/index.php?action=carro/showProduct&itmId=9726&rbrId=139)*

Founded by Veronika Burian and José Scaglione, type foundry [TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com) has published its new book titled “[Building Ligatures: The Power of Type](https://www.type-together.com/index.php?action=carro/showProduct&itmId=9726&rbrId=139)”. Drawing from the idea that a ligature creates connections between characters resulting in something new, useful, and evocative, the book offers a cross-section of the foundry's knowledge, experience, and best practices in type design, typography, and graphic design over the fifteen years since its founding. 🇬🇧

## Roboto Flex, Google Fonts’ most popular typeface, has been released.

*Image: © [Google Fonts](https://material.io/blog/roboto-flex)*

A new member named [Roboto Flex](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Roboto+Flex) has been added to Roboto, Google Fonts’ most popular type family first released in 2011. Featuring a wide range of weights and widths across optical sizes, as well as additional capabilities for fine-tuning, Roboto Flex was designed by Font Bureau. Taking full advantage of the possibilities of variable font technology, Roboto Flex stands out through its masterful use of [parametric axes](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/parametric_axis). 🇬🇧

## Who needs words when you have symbols anyway?

*Image: © [AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/who-%E2%9D%8B-needs-words-%E2%9C%8E-when-you-%E2%9E%B0have-symbols%E2%9D%93/)*

A worthwhile [article](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/who-%E2%9D%8B-needs-words-%E2%9C%8E-when-you-%E2%9E%B0have-symbols%E2%9D%93/) by Elizabeth Goodspeed on the growing trend of replacing words with symbols—which she terms the “Nouveau Rebus”—especially in branding projects. 🇬🇧 A [rebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebus), also known as [resfebe](https://tr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resfebe) in Turkish, refers to expressing words through writing, symbols, or images rather than their usual forms, or puzzles created in this way. Additionally, in the [October 2020 news](https://medium.com/tipografi/tipografi-haberleri-ekim-2020-a04d29bed91b), we shared Ece Çalış Zeğerek’s article titled “[A Narrative Language in Graphic Design: Rebus Design](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1197767)”.

## A new typeface from Dinamo: Marist

*Image: © [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com/typefaces/marist)*

[Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com) announced [Marist](https://abcdinamo.com/typefaces/marist), a new typeface designed by Seb McLauchlan. First developed in the 1470s by Nicolas Jenson and later reinterpreted in the 19th century by William Morris under the name [Golden Type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Type) to celebrate the craft aesthetic of the past, Marist is a humanist typeface with pronounced serifs and wide proportions that stands out for being reader-friendly. [Seb McLauchlan](https://sebmclauchlan.com) is an independent designer originally from New Zealand who has been living in London since 2015.

## The type designs of Rudolf Koch

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://youtu.be/HCpMYO_Sgrg)*

A [presentation](https://youtu.be/HCpMYO_Sgrg) by Dr. Dorothee Ader, director of the [Klingspor Museum](https://www.klingspor-museum.de), on the type designs, letterings, and drawings of German type designer, calligrapher, and illustrator [Rudolf Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Koch) (1876–1934). 🇬🇧 Having spent his youth working as an apprentice in a metal workshop in Hanau, Koch later began studying at the Nuremberg Academy of Fine Arts. Between 1897 and 1906, he worked at various businesses related to bookselling in Leipzig. He designed book covers in the Art Nouveau style, the popular movement of the time. In 1906, he began working at the Rudhard Foundry in Offenbach—which later became the Klingspor Type Foundry—where prominent designers such as Otto Eckmann and Peter Behrens worked. Known for typefaces such as [Neuland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuland) and [Kabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabel_(typeface)) created for the Klingspor Type Foundry, Koch passed away from a heart attack in 1934 at the age of just 57.

## Which type foundries support font subsetting?

*Image: © [subsetting.xyz](https://subsetting.xyz)*

[Jeff Frankl](https://frankl.org) has [compiled](https://subsetting.xyz) a list of type foundries that enable font subsetting, a technique that can reduce font file sizes on the web by approximately 60%. [Subsetting](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/subsetting) is the process of generating a file that contains a custom, usually limited character set of a font.
