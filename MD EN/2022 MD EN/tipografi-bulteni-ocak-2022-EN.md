## Boğos Tatikyan, painter and printer from Izmir

*Image: © [Painter Boğos Tatikyan](https://kulturyayinlari.izka.org.tr/tatikyann)*

The book about Boğos Tatikyan, a painter and printer from Izmir, prepared by our founding member [Ömer Durmaz](https://twitter.com/omerdurmaz) and İskender Dereli, has been published by the Izmir Development Agency. Boğos Tatikyan, a painter and printer from Izmir, depicted Ottoman sultans, ordinary people he observed in and around the city of Izmir, and particularly intriguing figures from different communities, reproducing them using the lithography (stone printing) technique in his own printing house and offering them for sale. The lithographs, printed in single color on single sheets, were hand-colored with watercolors using a brush, and printed in portable and storable sizes. The book on Tatikyan, who played an important role in providing access to colorful visual sources regarding the folklore and history of Ottoman Izmir, can be read online [here](https://kulturyayinlari.izka.org.tr/tatikyann).

## A new book from Dori Griffin offering an overview of type specimens

*Image: © [Bloomsbury](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/type-specimens-9781350116610/)*

“[Type Specimens: A Visual History of Typesetting and Printing](https://www.amazon.com/Type-Specimens-History-Typesetting-Printing/dp/1350116599/ref=sr_1_1?crid=18S6Z4KKD8KAW&keywords=Dori+Griffin&qid=1642073201&sprefix=%2Caps%2C1240&sr=8-1)”, the new book by [Prof. Dori Griffin](https://arts.ufl.edu/directory/profile/166907) from the University of Florida, offers an overview of type specimens published since the 1480s. Featuring more than 200 full-color type specimens from 24 countries and 15 different alphabets, the book investigates the process of type specimens that shaped today’s design landscape and facilitated global typographic exchange. While the e-book edition of the book published by Bloomsbury is currently available for purchase, the print edition will go on sale in the coming days.

## Interesting research on the evolution of a rare African script

*Image: © [shh.mpg.de](https://www.shh.mpg.de/2089042/evolution-of-writing)*

[An interesting article](https://www.shh.mpg.de/2089042/evolution-of-writing) on the evolution of the Vai script as a model of how writing systems progress from complex to simpler forms. Based on the analysis of the Vai script, an isolated West African writing system, in a study published in the journal [Current Anthropology](https://www.jstor.org/journal/curranth) by a research team from the [Max Planck Institute](https://www.shh.mpg.de/en) in Jena, Germany, the writing system evolves to become simpler and more efficient. 🇬🇧

## The design process of TDC Award of Excellence winner Oi

*Image: © [designyatra.com](https://designyatra.com/the-story-of-an-endearing-typeface-called-oi/)*

[Kostas Bartsokas](https://kostasbartsokas.com) has [shared](https://designyatra.com/the-story-of-an-endearing-typeface-called-oi/) the design process of the typeface [Oi](https://kostasbartsokas.com/oi-you-mate/), which won the TDC Award of Excellence in Type Design in 2018. Rooted in the slab serif style that emerged with British typographer Henry Caslon’s 1844 *Ionic* and the London-based [Fann Street Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fann_Street_Foundry)’s 1845 *Clarendon*, Oi is available via [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Oi). 🇬🇧

## A new publication from Underware

*Image: © [Underware](https://underware.nl/publications/manicule_specimen/)*

Founded in 1999, popular type foundry [Underware](https://underware.nl) stands out not only for its pioneering work, but also for the rich [publications](https://underware.nl/publications/) it brings to the typography world. Most recently, the type foundry announced [Manicule Specimen](https://underware.nl/publications/manicule_specimen/), an illustrated publication that briefly recounts the transition of the pointing hand (☞, [manicule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manicule)) from the margins of 12th-century books to contemporary typefaces, revealing their love for meticulously designed pointing hands. Comprising 16 pages, the publication is, rather than a type specimen, a *pointing hand specimen* where each pointing hand is presented alongside the typeface to which it belongs.

## Winners announced in the 2022 Typography Competition.

*Image: © [Communication Arts](https://twitter.com/ordertypefound/status/1483864581772619785)*

In the [typography competition](https://www.commarts.com/competition/2022-typography) organized by Communication Arts magazine, the typeface [Plebeian](https://order.design/otf/plebeian) from Brooklyn-based [Order Type Foundry](https://order.design/otf)—founded by Emily Klaebe and Jesse Reed, known for their branding work for Kickstarter and MoMA—was selected as the winner. In the competition open to type designs published between September 2020 and September 2021, with a jury including names like Petra Dočekalová, Nina Stössinger, and Liza Enebeis, Plebeian, designed by [Benjamin Tuttle](http://www.btuttle.com), stands out as a contemporary sans serif text typeface based on Frank E. Blokland’s research into the modularity of Latin letterforms. You can access the shortlist of type designs [here](https://www.commarts.com/content/files/competitions/Type22Shortlist.pdf). 🇬🇧

## A female president for ISTD

[Astrid Stavro](https://twitter.com/AstridStavro) [has become](https://www.instagram.com/p/CZPSigDMoE2/) the new president of the International Society of Typographic Designers ([ISTD](https://www.istd.org.uk)). After studying philosophy and literature at Boston University, Stavro completed her graphic design education at Central Saint Martins. She later attended the Royal College of Art. There, she met Jeff Willis, who influenced her education and gave her a humble perspective on life and graphic design. Co-founder of Design by Atlas, Stavro was elected a member of the Alliance Graphique Internationale (AGI) in 2010. Between 2018 and 2021, she was a partner at Pentagram. Stavro is cited as an exemplar of the idea of adopting an outward-looking global perspective within the discipline.

## On the nostalgic power of typography in The French Dispatch

*Image: © [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/features/monotypes-marie-boulanger-on-the-nostalgic-power-of-typography-in-the-french-dispatch/)*

From director Wes Anderson’s previous films such as [The Grand Budapest Hotel](https://www.typeroom.eu/article/why-grand-budapest-hotel-s-typography-star-movie) and [Moonrise Kingdom](https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/moonrise-kingdom/), we already knew that his productions featured remarkably strong lettering, typography, and graphic treatments. In Anderson’s latest film [The French Dispatch](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt8847712/), released in 2021, the typefaces and letterforms open a window into the country’s rich history as nostalgic, artistic elements and characters in their own right. To uncover all the typographic splendor of the film and what we can learn from it, Dom Carter spoke with type designer [Marie Boulanger](http://marie-boulanger.com) from Monotype. 🇬🇧
