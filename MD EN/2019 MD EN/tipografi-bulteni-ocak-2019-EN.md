## “How Aldus Manutius Saved Civilization?”

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aui8IXBVoGY&fbclid=IwAR3H5KCZc4V_ZZtfPji_FZgb_5ntULD27H_mK0GRLRUzdhIhwL0KUxAPXiw)*

G. Scott Clemons’s 2015 talk on Aldus Manutius at a [Type@Cooper](http://coopertype.org) event: [“How Aldus Manutius Saved Civilization?”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aui8IXBVoGY&fbclid=IwAR3H5KCZc4V_ZZtfPji_FZgb_5ntULD27H_mK0GRLRUzdhIhwL0KUxAPXiw) (Eng.)

Clemons argues that Aldus’s striking decision at the end of the 15th century to abandon a scholarly life led out of love for literature and books and move to Venice, plunging into the cutthroat trade of the printing world, continues to reverberate across modern fields such as textual criticism, book design, typography, book production, copyright law, collecting, and classical philology.

Clemons’s choice of talk title is also significant. He emphasizes that Aldus’s decision helped bring the Greek canon into the present day by printing ancient Greek works with movable type, thereby committing the surviving works to print for the first time.

> We also recommend reading on our [Medium](https://medium.com/tipografi) page the translated article titled [Typefaces as History: Aldus Manutius and the Noble Bembo](https://medium.com/tipografi/tarih-olarak-yazıyüzleri-aldus-manutius-ve-asil-bembo-a03ef4e89c0c?fbclid=IwAR1CEUazQInxiZZuGMBhXCGdu7rs4hoCRiEs0NUA49nbGh9afpU4kH-9CUU).

## The thickness of a horizontal line is perceived to be greater than that of a vertical line.

*Image: © [www.mdpi.com/2411-5150/3/1/1/htm](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5150/3/1/1/htm)*

In an [article](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5150/3/1/1/htm) published in the journal *Vision*, Jasper M. de Waard, Erik Van der Burg, and Christian N. L. Olivers present two psychophysical experiments investigating a visual illusion that is a common consensus among type designers but had never been scientifically studied. According to the experimental results, the thickness of a horizontal line is perceived to be greater compared to that of a vertical line.

## Optician Sans, based on the same principles as the LogMAR chart

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7U0riH6cQG8)*

[Optician Sans](https://www.behance.net/gallery/73154571/OPTICIAN-SANS), based on the same principles as the LogMAR chart developed by the *National Vision Research Institute of Australia* to measure visual acuity, has been announced. Serving as a continuation of the 1862 Snellen and 1959 Sloan charts, Optician Sans was designed by Fábio Duarte Martins and ANTI Hamar.

An article by Fábio Duarte Martins, one of the designers of Optician Sans, on how optical fonts are developed, titled [Optotypes? No, Optician Sans! ](http://bit.ly/2H3BoFh). (Eng.)

## A new typeface from Lineto: LL Bradford

*Image: © [lineto.com](https://lineto.com)*

Founded in 1993 by Cornel Windlin and Stephan Müller, the Swiss type foundry [Lineto](http://lineto.com) has introduced its first serif typeface, [LL Bradford](http://bit.ly/2VCFh7y). Designed by Laurenz Brunner, LL Bradford features fourteen weights.

## Fatih Hardal’s award-winning typeface design: Nişantaşı

The typeface design “[Nişantaşı](http://bit.ly/2Fm6aqH)” by Fatih Hardal, which was awarded the “Best Student Project Award” at the 37th Graphic Design Awards organized by the Turkish Graphic Designers Association (GMK) in 2018.

Noted to be inspired by Hubert Jocham’s type family *Narziss*, the four-weight typeface draws attention with its neoclassical decorative structure.

The first installment of [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com)’s ten-part video and article series aiming to answer a simple question, [“Good Type Respects Its Elders.](http://bit.ly/2sjRBMl)” has been published. The series offers an in-depth look at different aspects of type design to help designers understand how to choose the right font for a specific project.

In the first episode of the series, [Jamie Neely](http://twitter.com/jamneely) emphasizes that nearly every font has a history behind its creation.

## Updated Bodoni: Bodoni*

*Image: © [Indestructible Fonts](https://indestructibletype.com)*

[Indestructible Fonts](https://indestructibletype.com) released the [Bodoni*](http://bit.ly/2CTGfUe) family, consisting of 56 weights in total. Created by [Giambattista Bodoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_Bodoni) and serving as a revision for the digital age of [Bodoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodoni)—one of the first modern serif typefaces—the type family is Open Font Licensed (OFL) and also features OpenType capabilities.
