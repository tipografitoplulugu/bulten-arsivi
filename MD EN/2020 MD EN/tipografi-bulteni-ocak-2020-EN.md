## Edward Johnston documentary from London Underground

*Image: © [Edward Johnston: A Memorial in Type](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LIAxVW-9fRo&feature=youtu.be)*

In recent months, large wooden letters were installed at Farringdon Station to commemorate the contribution to the city's memory made by [Edward Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Johnston), creator of the London Underground’s famous typeface [Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_(typeface))—which we also shared with our readers. Not only did London Underground create a memorial artwork, but they also released a short [documentary](https://youtu.be/LIAxVW-9fRo) to commemorate Edward Johnston.

## Gimlet X-Ray, showing how a variable font works

*Image: © [Gimlet X-Ray.](https://djr.com/gimlet-xray/)*

Created by [David Jonathan Ross](https://twitter.com/djrrb/), [Gimlet X-Ray](https://djr.com/gimlet-xray/) demonstrates how variable fonts work by revealing the inner mechanics of the font across the weight and width axes. Featuring the control points and Bézier curves that shape the letterforms, Gimlet X-Ray also showcases spacing and kerning information.

## Are typefaces political?

*Image: © Yello*

In his [article](https://yello.substack.com/p/do-fonts-have-a-political-party), Hunter Schwarz examines the political connotations of typefaces through the study titled [What’s in a Font?: Ideological Perceptions of Type](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10510974.2019.1692884?fbclid=IwAR3OSZisB0vhWnWLQimJY0LmJ0mv5UrT3f71h1QhY-aamzB9JPbyr4cxnes#.Xg9tFaHNYKQ.twitter), published in December 2019 in the journal *Communications Studies* by Virginia Tech professors Katherine Haenschen and Daniel J. Tamul. The study conducted with 987 participants reveals that typefaces carry ideological leanings, with serif and bold type conveying a conservative impression, while sans serif and italic type give a liberal one.

Lilly Smith’s [article](https://www.fastcompany.com/90458816/the-real-politics-of-type) published in [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com) regarding the same research.

## Studio Feixen released its first type family.

*Image: © [Studio Feixen](https://fonts.studiofeixen.ch)*

Based in Lucerne, Switzerland, [Studio Feixen](https://www.studiofeixen.ch) [released](https://fonts.studiofeixen.ch) its first type family. Originally designed for use in the studio’s visual assets, the superfamily is presented to designers in three distinct variants, including variable font versions in both serif and sans serif. The [Studio Feixen Fonts](https://fonts.studiofeixen.ch) website, launched to introduce the type family, also signals the arrival of new typefaces.

## Monotype acquired renowned type foundry Fontsmith.

*Image: © Monotype*

[Monotype](https://monotype.com) [announced](https://monotype.com/resources/monotype-acquires-fontsmith) that it has acquired London-based type foundry [Fontsmith](https://www.fontsmith.com). As a result, Monotype’s portfolio expands to include 70 type families, including Fontsmith’s popular typefaces [FS Albert](https://www.fontsmith.com/fonts/fs-albert), [FS Brabo](https://www.fontsmith.com/fonts/fs-brabo), [FS Koopman](https://www.fontsmith.com/fonts/fs-koopman), [FS Meridian](https://www.fontsmith.com/fonts/fs-meridian), [FS Millbank](http://www.fsmillbank.com/#/fs-millbank), [FS Split](https://www.fontsmith.com/bundles/fs-split-bundle), and [FS Untitled ](https://www.fontsmith.com/fonts/fs-untitled).

Founded in London in 1997 by [Jason Smith](https://twitter.com/fsjason), Fontsmith recently made headlines by designing one of the world’s [first variable font logos](https://www.fontsmith.com/blog/2019/06/17/fontsmith-and-vbat-create-the-world-s-first-variable-font-logo) for the WPP campus in Amsterdam, and introduced a [collection of nine variable fonts](https://www.fontsmith.com/blog/2019/10/29/fontsmith-variable-fonts-have-landed) in October.
