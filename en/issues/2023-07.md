## Custom typeface for Beşiktaş

*Image: © [Beşiktaş](https://twitter.com/Besiktas/status/1672303737438609422)*

Beşiktaş [announced](https://twitter.com/Besiktas/status/1672303737438609422) the typeface named *I am Beşiktaş*, which it commissioned for use in UEFA Europa Conference League group and friendly matches. Abroad, we frequently see meticulously crafted examples where sports clubs commission custom type designs. Most recently, La Liga's visual identity, which also includes its new typeface, was introduced in Spain. In England, the new typeface prepared for the Premier League’s 2023–2024 Season in collaboration with [Avery Dennison](https://www.averydennison.com/en/home.html) was announced with a [comprehensive documentary](https://youtu.be/F7UO0eVSqmM) featuring the design and production processes, tests conducted with league commentators, and various interviews. Even though we cannot see this level of meticulousness in the Beşiktaş example, it is pleasing that such an initiative exists. ☞ **Bonus: **Book Recommendation** “[https://f37foundry.com/goods/football-type-2](https://f37foundry.com/goods/football-type-2)**Football Type 2”

## How typeface choice changes the way we read and write?

*Image: © [washingtonpost.com](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2023/best-font-you-test-types-styles/)*

A wonderful [article](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2023/best-font-you-test-types-styles/) was published in The Washington Post showing how some typefaces are read more easily and quickly than others. Readers can choose the typeface they want from the list to see which one is more suitable for them. 🇬🇧

☞** Note: **The statement in the article “X height — literally the height of a lowercase letter x” is problematic because [x-height](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/glossary/x_height) is not *strictly* the height of the letter x. **Although x-height is based on the height of the letter x, it encompasses the height of lowercase letters that have neither descenders nor ascenders.**

## The story of type designs from Frere-Jones

*Image: © [Frere-Jones](https://frerejones.com/blog/designing-surveyor)*

> “Typefaces can carry stories best when they have a story of their own.”

We care about the stories and design processes of typefaces, and we frequently feature articles on this subject in our bulletin. Frere-Jones Type Foundry announced that they have started a new article series to accurately record the stories of typefaces, following Monotype’s acquisition of Hoefler&Co and the typefaces designed by Tobias Frere-Jones in 2021. Compiled by [Doug Wilson](https://twitter.com/realdougwilson), the first articles of the series cover the design process of the typefaces named [Surveyor](https://www.myfonts.com/collections/surveyor-font-hoefler-and-co) and [Whitney](https://frerejones.com/blog/designing-whitney). 🇬🇧

## Printing press calligraphy in the Ottoman Empire

*Image: An invoice sample from Hamid Yazı Yurdu © [Mehmet Erken](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/fsmia/issue/78189/1318923)*

[Mehmet Erken](https://twitter.com/MehmetEIU)’s article titled “[Writing on Stone: Printing Press Calligraphy in the Ottoman Empire](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/fsmia/issue/78189/1318923)” has been published. The article explores the journey of printing press calligraphers in the Ottoman Empire between the years 1831–1928 up until the alphabet reform. Addressing a similar topic, Ayşe Başaran, in her [article](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/divan/issue/78768/1253075) published in the journal *Divan*, examines the central role of the ulema and calligraphers—commonly known as opponents of the printing press—in the proliferation of printing presses. 🇬🇧

## Steven Heller on brush sign painting

*Image: © [Letterform Archive](https://letterformarchive.org/news/w-g-sutherland-the-sign-writer-and-glass-embosser-manchester-london-1898/)*

Brush sign painting continues to appear before us with ever-growing interest. In his column titled *The Daily Heller* published on Print magazine's website, Steven Heller wrote an [article](https://www.printmag.com/daily-heller/the-daily-heller-sign-writing-and-glass-engraving/) on brush sign painting featuring examples from William George Sutherland’s 1895 book [The Sign Writer and Glass Embosser](https://archive.org/details/SutherlandSignWriter). 🇬🇧 In his article, Heller notes that work that once took days or weeks can now be done in a much shorter time, but questions whether the results are better than the examples showcased in the book.

## A new typeface from Erik Spiekermann: Case

*Image: © [Fontwerk](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/case-superfamily)*

Erik Spiekermann, Anja Meiners, and Ralph du Carrois released their new typeface named [Case](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/case-superfamily) through [Fontwerk](https://fontwerk.com/en). A *neo-grotesque* typeface, Case is a complete superfamily comprising three typefaces: Case, Case Text, and Case Micro.

## Monotype continues to monopolize.

*Image: © [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com/company/press-release/fontworks-plans-to-join-monotype)*

In our past bulletins, we drew attention to Monotype’s monopolization in the type industry by covering its acquisitions. Following Monotype’s acquisition of Berthold’s typefaces and renowned type foundries such as Fontsmith, URW, and Hoefler&Co, a comprehensive [article](https://qz.com/2068310/what-monotypes-purchase-of-hoeflerco-means-to-font-designers/) was published on the monopolization of the type industry; Nuclear Bits [evaluated](https://nuclearbits.com/bye-hoefler-monotype-hello-commissioner/) the impact of the Hoefler&Co acquisition on their company. Typographica also voiced its reaction by making a [post](https://twitter.com/typographica/status/1563262628322873347/photo/1) stating that most users have no idea that the vast majority of the type industry is controlled by a single company. Most recently, Monotype [announced](https://www.monotype.com/company/press-release/fontworks-plans-to-join-monotype) that it has agreed to acquire [Fontworks](https://en.fontworks.co.jp/), the iconic Tokyo-based type foundry that has contributed to shaping type culture in Japan over the last thirty years. Notable as Monotype’s first acquisition in Japan, the deal is slated to close in the third quarter of 2023. 🇬🇧

## Twitter’s logo has changed!

*Image: © [Elon Musk](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1682978324375543808)*

Elon Musk [announced](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1683877911315599360?s=20) Twitter’s new logo. Even though experts [warned](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/jul/24/elon-musk-reveals-the-new-twitter-logo-x) that rebranding could be a risky move, the new logo began to be used. Musk had attempted to rebrand PayPal under the same name in 2000, but was [ousted](https://www.disconnect.blog/p/elon-musk-wants-to-relive-his-start) by the board of directors. Musk’s choice of a Unicode character numbered [U+1D54F](https://www.compart.com/en/unicode/U+1D54F), designed for use by mathematicians, as the new logo was widely debated. Although the new logo was [compared](https://twitter.com/willphoto/status/1684036856646868992) to the character X in Monotype’s typeface *Special Alphabet 4*, it was [clarified](https://twitter.com/monotype/status/1683836498175750144) that Monotype’s creative director Phil Garnham examined the logo and that there is no similarity to the character in the aforementioned typeface. Louis Ashworth from the Financial Times wrote a detailed [article](https://www.ft.com/content/da262b2a-f39a-466b-9b2f-2f8fa84f0117) on the matter. Gerry Leonidas, on the other hand, shared his thoughts via his [LinkedIn account](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gerryleonidas_yesterday-i-got-asked-by-a-journalist-a-few-activity-7089627589744435200-JC_q/). According to him, based on absolute criteria, the new logo is a bad design—especially for digital environments—and conceptually weak. 🇬🇧

## Albert Boton has passed away.

*Image: Albert Boton (1932–2023) © [Fontstand](https://fontstand.com/news/design-news/albert-boton-19322023/#french-version)*

French type designer Albert Boton (1932–2023) has [passed away](https://fontstand.com/news/design-news/albert-boton-19322023/). 🇬🇧 In 1952, while apprenticing alongside his carpenter father, Boton discovered graphic design when he visited the advertising agency Atelier Troy in Paris to replace its window. He enrolled at École Estienne, where he took classes from Adrian Frutiger. Following his education, he joined the Deberny & Peignot Type Foundry, where he had the opportunity to work alongside figures such as Adrian Frutiger, Ladislas Mandel, and Lucette Girard. Achieving international success with Eras in 1961, Boton worked for Paris's largest advertising agencies and created numerous type designs such as Elan, Carrénoir, Scherzo, Pompei, and Linex Sans.
