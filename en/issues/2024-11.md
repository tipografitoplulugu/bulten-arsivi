# Typography Newsletter: November 2024

### In this month's bulletin, there are significant developments in typography from around the world and Turkey.

⏰ **Reading Time:** ~6 minutes

Hello everyone!

Featured news, new typefaces, and developments from the typography world in the Turkish Typography Society’s November 2024 bulletin:

---

## News from Words of Type

[In the September 2023 bulletin](https://medium.com/tipografi/tipografi-b%C3%BClteni-eyl%C3%BCl-2023-92014965a49a), — you can subscribe for free [here](https://medium.com/tipografi/newsletter) so you don't miss out on developments in the typography world — we mentioned the [Words of Type](https://www.wordsoftype.com/) project, initiated by Type Designer Lisa Huang to create an online, multilingual encyclopedia of typographic terms. There is new news from the project. It held its [first event](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9CXOcxPbJVA) on September 7, 2024, themed around vocabulary in typography, with the participation of David Berlow, Eric Q. Liu, Tânia Raposo, Nick Sherman, and Mariko Takagi. [Words of Type](https://www.wordsoftype.com/) also organizes paid educational events. Workshops primarily focused on non-Latin scripts and type design in these scripts can be accessed on the project's website.

## On typography with Ulrike Rausch

An [interview](https://www.callingmag.com/gelenek-ve-yenilik-ulrike-rausch-ile-tipografi-uzerine/?fbclid=PAZXh0bgNhZW0CMTEAAab5S15fOOCa5a_tKCLXpWdymhzMcFGh86Ci_-1MsFRhAL7xgEk3nTkDVpc_aem_TltNyV_nm70_AZ_WnStpNw) by Büşra Soydemir from Calling Mag with [Ulrike Rausch](https://liebefonts.com/about), one of the speakers at this year's [ISType](https://istype.com/2024/) conference. In her interview, Rausch discusses the technical depths of type design, the dynamic power that coding brings to design, and how she brings the potential of font technologies to life in creative projects.

## Custom type designs by Typotheque

Typotheque stands out not only for the typefaces in its library, but also for the custom typefaces it crafts for projects where existing fonts fall short. The design processes of the typefaces designed by the type foundry for the [Paris Transit System](https://www.typotheque.com/articles/typeface-for-paris-metro)—used by more than a billion people a year—the vehicle interfaces and branding requirements of [Ford Motors](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/new-fonts-for-ford-motors), and the visual identity of the [Monterey Bay Aquarium](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/monterey-bay-aquarium-custom-fonts) renewed by Pentagram, are well worth examining.

---

## A typeface dedicated to the legacy of Formula 1

A custom typeface named *Torque* was [designed](https://type-01.com/torque-a-custom-typeface-drives-formula-1s-legacy-at-londons-latest-exhibition/) by [Hingston Studio](https://www.hingston.studio/) for the visual identity of the [exhibition](https://f1exhibition.com/), which features a layered narrative of the evolution of Formula 1, ranging from rare archival footage to full-scale car models and engineering marvels. The typeface is a dynamic blend of modernity and nostalgia across three fonts named *Torque 100, Torque 50*, and *Torque Inline*.

---

## A new typeface for Rolling Stone from Commercial Type

Commercial Type designed a customized slab serif for the famed Rolling Stone magazine, merging both the vintage and modern aspects of the publication while emphasizing legibility across different media. The typeface named [Rolling Stone Slab](https://commercialtype.com/custom/new_type_for_rolling_stone) is based on the development work of [Successor](https://commercialtype.com/catalog/successor), designed by Tim Ripper.

---

## “The Automatic Type Design Conference”

[The Automatic Type Design Conference](https://automatic-type-design.anrt-nancy.fr/colloques/automatic-type-design-3), the third edition of which will be organized next year, will take place in Nancy, France, from February 17–21, 2025. Organized by ANRT/ENSAD in partnership with ÉCAL, the conference aims to discuss type technology from historical, contemporary, and experimental perspectives.

---

## An exhibition on Manuzio and the book revolution

Organized as a collaborative effort between Venice International University and Sabancı University on Aldo Manuzio (Aldus Manutius), one of the most prominent figures in the history of printing and publishing, the exhibition [opened](https://x.com/sabanciuni_fass/status/1853729832254710055) at the FASS Art Gallery between November 6–29, 2024. Featuring design contributions from Selçuk Artut and Onur Yazıcıgil from Turkey, the exhibition had met with audiences last month at the Frankfurt Book Fair.

---

## Touching Memory and the City Through Intuitive Typography

As part of the “Dialogue” events of [İyi Tasarım İzmir](https://www.iyitasarimizmir.org/), a workshop titled “[Emotional Cycles: Touching Memory and the City Through Intuitive Typography](https://www.instagram.com/p/DB8pAOOIhyB/)” was held on November 9, 2024, coordinated by Assoc. Prof. Dr. Dicle Yıldırım. Aiming to establish a dialectical bond between emotions and the city through typography, the workshop sought to question the sounds, visuals, and sensations in collective memory through intuitive approaches and the associations of letters within the dialogue between the city’s internal and external dynamics.

---

## International Anatolian Calligraphy and Typography Event

The [International Anatolian Calligraphy and Typography Event](https://kaligrafitipografi.anadolu.edu.tr/), held for the sixteenth time this year, took place between November 11–24, 2024. Held at the Anadolu University Campus, the event featured a conference on November 11 with the participation of Kenan Temizel, Banu Bulduk Türkmen, İpek Torun, Gaye Kalavlı, and Erhan Olcay, as well as workshops between November 12–15. At the same time, the *Yunus Emre* exhibition welcomed visitors at the Contemporary Arts Museum on campus.

---

## Barın Han events

[Barın Han](https://barinhan.com/), the studio of Turkey’s leading calligrapher and bookbinding artist Prof. Emin Barın, hosted a [talk](https://www.instagram.com/p/DC1NxzJofRA/) on Saturday, November 30, regarding the installation titled “Life is Like a Tooth” designed by Gülizar Çepoğlu in memory of French polymath Boris Vian. In her talk, artist and curator Margarita Osepyan explained how new technologies question old technologies and traditional ways of thinking through letterpress and post-digital printing, as well as applications and research in two-dimensional and three-dimensional typographic spaces.

---

## “An Essay on Typography” in Turkish!

Eric Gill’s (1882–1940) book titled “An Essay on Typography”, considered a classic since its publication in 1931, has been [published](https://www.kitapyurdu.com/kitap/tipografi-ustune-bir-deneme/698064.html) by Liberus Publications in a translation by Ertuğrul Pek. In the book, where Gill discusses the development of modern typography while examining the balance between the importance of craftsmanship and industrial production, he offers a technical and philosophical perspective.

## New book from Counter-Print: “Expressive Type Today”

Counter-Print’s latest book, “[Expressive Type Today](https://www.counter-print.co.uk/collections/counterprinttitles/products/expressive-type-today)”, celebrates the enduring power of typography to captivate, provoke, and inspire, showcasing the boundless creativity of those who use type as a medium of expression.

## A comprehensive type specimen from Commercial Type

Pre-orders have opened for “[Double Acts in Pop](https://commercialtype.com/goods/double_acts_in_pop)”, Commercial Type’s new publication and their first comprehensive type specimen in nearly a decade, presenting their entire library in a book format.

## A magazine on experimental typography: Plain Text

Published by Plain Form, [Plain Text](https://text.plain-form.com/en) is a new magazine on experimental typography that aims to explore typographic visions, transcend historical and technical boundaries, and awaken the unknown and the dormant potential of type.

## A Chinese newspaper’s composing room from the late 1920s

[Photographs](https://typography-daily.com/blog/2024/11/19/a-chinese-newspapers-composing-room-from-the-late-1920s/) taken by Paul Hutchinson in 1927 showing the composing room of a Chinese newspaper in the late 1920s. Typesetting in Chinese characters is exceedingly difficult due to the number of characters it contains. It is estimated that a compositor had to walk more than three miles to typeset a single page.
