## Uzbekistan is switching to the Latin alphabet.

*Image: © [Karar](https://www.karar.com/ozbekistan-latin-alfabesine-geciyor-1605313)*

Following Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan has also decided to switch to the Latin alphabet. [According to the report covered in Prof. Dr. Kürşad Zorlu’s column](https://www.haberturk.com/yazarlar/prof-dr-kursad-zorlu/2988416-kazakistandan-sonra-ozbekistan-da-latin-alfabesi-karari-aldi), starting January 1, 2023, all activities and official correspondence of institutions and organizations in the country will be conducted in the Latin alphabet. All press/media outlets, electronic media, news websites, printing houses, and publishing houses in the country will also use the Latin alphabet. Uzbekistan had initiated its first transition efforts to the Latin alphabet in 1993 following the dissolution of the USSR, but the Cyrillic alphabet continued to be widely used.

## Proxima Vara: The variable version of Proxima Nova

*Image: © [proxima-vara.marksimonson.com](http://proxima-vara.marksimonson.com)*

[Proxima Vara](https://proxima-vara.marksimonson.com), the variable version of [Mark Simonson](https://www.marksimonson.com)’s famous typeface [Proxima Nova](https://www.marksimonson.com/fonts/view/proxima-nova), has been released. The famous type family, which Simonson began sketching in the early 1980s and emerged under the name *Visigothic* in the early 1990s, was announced to the public in 1994 under the name Proxima Sans. In 2002, the rising popularity of Gotham created a new demand for geometric typefaces, paving the way for Proxima Sans. That same year, Rolling Stone magazine began using Proxima Sans as part of its redesign led by Matthew Ball. Proxima Nova, the revised version of Proxima Sans, was commercially released for the first time in 2005.

## Quality and accessible fonts for everyone

*Image: © [indiantypefoundry.com](http://www.indiantypefoundry.com/)*

[Indian Type Foundry (ITF)](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com) introduced [Fontshare](https://www.fontshare.com), a service that makes high-quality typefaces accessible to everyone. With open-source typefaces such as [Poppins](https://www.indiantypefoundry.com/fonts/poppins), which has become quite popular in recent years, as well as [Rajdhani](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Rajdhani?preview.text_type=custom), [Khand](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Khand), [Hind](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Hind), and [Teko](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Teko?preview.text_type=custom), Indian Type Foundry had begun offering its entire type library free of charge to students, design institutions, and other non-profit organizations. Launched with the goal of helping brands and individuals who lack a budget but still want access to beautiful typography, Fontshare is an extension of this philosophy.

## A brief history of ATypI

*Image: © [ATypI](https://medium.com/atypi-notes/atypi-history-draft-a6c955671100)*

[John D. Berry](https://johndberry.com), who served as president of [ATypI](https://www.atypi.org) (Association Typographique Internationale) between 2007 and 2013, shared the [first post](https://medium.com/atypi-notes/atypi-history-draft-a6c955671100) of a short book series documenting ATypI’s history from its founding in 1957 to the present day. The book provides an overview of ATypI’s origins, how and why it came about, who was involved, and some of the ups and downs of its early years. It is noted that future posts will focus on how ATypI developed after the first decade, in-depth examinations of specific topics, specific projects of ATypI’s work, and some of the people influential in both ATypI and the typographic community.

## A new book from Christopher Burke

[Christopher Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Burke_(design_writer)) has published his book documenting the type design career of [Gerard Unger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Unger), one of the pioneers of Dutch graphic design whom we lost in 2018, with examples. Titled [Gerard Unger: Life in letters](https://www.uitgeverijdebuitenkant.nl/grafisch_ontwerpen/#307), the book presents a case study of Unger’s role in the development of modern type design, alongside the technological shifts that have transformed typography over the past fifty years.

## Hyperglot: A database detecting language support in fonts

*Image: © [Hyperglot](https://hyperglot.rosettatype.com)*

[Rosetta Type Foundry](https://www.rosettatype.com) announced [Hyperglot](https://hyperglot.rosettatype.com), which provides information on which languages a font supports and approximately how many people speak that language. Developed in Python and presenting the standard character set used by a language, [Hyperglot](https://hyperglot.rosettatype.com) is an ongoing open-source project with a database covering more than 640 languages.
