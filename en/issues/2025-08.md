## GUS’25 Merit Award: Yaprak Buse Çağlar

*Image: © [TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com/2025-gerard-unger-scholarship-results)*

Within the scope of the [2025 Gerard Unger Scholarship](https://www.type-together.com/2025-gerard-unger-scholarship-results), this year’s merit award was presented to [Yaprak Buse Çağlar](https://www.instagram.com/typolea/) for the typeface [Aksan](https://esadtype.esad-amiens.fr/aksan/), which she designed at EsadType. Designed to cover many languages using the Latin alphabet, Aksan draws inspiration from the Turkish Alphabet Reform, blending powerful visual references of the past with today's design sensibility.

The main award of the scholarship was won by Manau Quellec with the type family [Newz](https://esadtype.esad-amiens.fr/newz/), developed under the mentorship of Sébastien Morlighem during his studies at Ésad Amiens. In the competition, where 47 designers from 16 different countries participated with a total of 49 type designs, TypeTogether co-founder Veronika Burian stated that Newz demonstrated strong potential in terms of conceptuality, execution, and craftsmanship quality, noting that it is a versatile and powerful type family especially for publications. José Scaglione expressed his praise with the words, “It looks so good it makes me want to get into publishing!”

## Manutius’s first edition printed in italic type sold for a record price!

*Image: © [sothebys.com](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2025/bibliotheca-brookeriana-a-renaissance-library-the-aldine-collection-n-z/vergilius-opera-venice-aldo-manuzio-1501?utm_source=chatgpt.com)*

A copy of Publius Vergilius Maro’s Opera from 1501, offered for sale at the auction of Bibliotheca Brookeriana, [found](https://www.sothebys.com/en/buy/auction/2025/bibliotheca-brookeriana-a-renaissance-library-the-aldine-collection-n-z/vergilius-opera-venice-aldo-manuzio-1501?utm_source=chatgpt.com) a buyer for a record price of $1,041,400. Printed by Aldus Manutius (1449–1515), the work is known not only as the first book in history printed in italic type, but also for introducing the *pocket classics* format to the publishing world. The italic type designed by Francesco Griffo, inspired by humanist cursive scripts, revolutionized the typographic approach of the era and served as a model for italic typefaces used in subsequent centuries. In this respect, the work holds an important place in terms of both printing history and typography.

## Hurufat Printing Museum granted “private museum” status!

*Image: © [Hurufat Printing Museum](https://hurufat.atauni.edu.tr/hurufat-baski-muzesi-ozel-muze-statusu-kazandi/)*

Opened on October 17, 2024, and holding the distinction of being Turkey’s largest and most comprehensive printing museum, the [Hurufat Printing Museum](https://hurufat.atauni.edu.tr/) was [registered](https://hurufat.atauni.edu.tr/hurufat-baski-muzesi-ozel-muze-statusu-kazandi/) with *private museum* status by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism as of this past June. Located in Erzurum and home to a rich collection comprising forty machines from the prepress, printing, and postpress stages, thousands of pieces of movable type, and various printing materials, the museum is open to visitors on weekdays from 10:00 to 16:00, except on public holidays.

## Crit’s design process

*Image: © [Element Type](https://elementtype.co/news/self-crit-on-biases-behind-forms/)*

We value the design processes behind typefaces and frequently feature them in our bulletins. Doğukan Karapınar, one of the founders of [Element Type](https://elementtype.co/), shared [a comprehensive article](https://elementtype.co/news/self-crit-on-biases-behind-forms/) on the design process of their recently released Crit. While developing Crit, references were drawn from the *inaccuracies* in the usage of Akzidenz Grotesk and Helvetica as well as the visual encounters prompted by these two typefaces; while preserving the classic grotesque heritage, an original expression was pursued through sharp terminals, short ascenders and descenders, and a uniqueness observed in every character.

## Alembert and the influence of the writing masters

*Image: © [Typofonderie](https://typofonderie.com/gazette/alembert-and-the-influence-of-the-maitres-ecrivains#fn1)*

Another design process is about [Alembert](https://typofonderie.com/gazette/alembert-and-the-influence-of-the-maitres-ecrivains#fn1), designed by Jean François Porchez for the thirtieth anniversary of [Typofonderie](https://typofonderie.com/)… In his article, Porchez highlights the influence of seventeenth-century French writing masters (*maîtres écrivains*) and explains how historical script styles like *Bâtarde* and *Coulée* are reinterpreted through contemporary typography. Alembert was created through a combination of digital tools and hand-drawing techniques, offering a more fluid and warm aesthetic compared to the classic Anglaise style. Thus, the typeface stands out as an original design bringing together both historical heritage and modern typographic needs.

☞ Used in France and its surroundings—especially Burgundy—in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, **Bâtarde** is a script style derived from blackletter, featuring more ornate and fluid lines. Coulée, on the other hand, is a semi-formal script style used in France in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries that provided speed and ease in daily life. By the nineteenth century, these styles gradually gave way to Anglaise (Copperplate, English Roundhand), which developed in England.

## New typeface from Dinamo: Areal

*Image: © [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com/newsletter/the-dinamo-update-areal-custom-typeface-arena)*

Dinamo Type Foundry has designed a custom typeface called [Areal](https://abcdinamo.com/newsletter/the-dinamo-update-areal-custom-typeface-arena) for the digital curation and idea-sharing platform *Are.na*. This new typeface was developed as a modern reinterpretation of Arial, born in the digital age, and aims to have an appearance almost indistinguishable from Arial. You can read the interview with Johannes Breyer, Charles Broskoski, and Meg Miller on the origin process and design philosophy of Areal [here](https://www.are.na/editorial/introducing-areal-are-nas-new-typeface).

In previous bulletins, we mentioned Typotheque’s work regarding the languages of Indigenous communities in North America. Following five years of research with First Nations and Inuit communities in Canada and the United States, the type foundry [announced](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/cherokee-osage-and-the-indigenous-north-american-type-collection) the release of new Cherokee and Osage typefaces. The new typefaces are accompanied by an extensive type collection supporting Indigenous communities in North America and their unique writing systems in terms of digital access, usage, and local typographic needs.

## TT Norms® Pro: The eight-year history of a type family

*Image: © [typetype.org](https://typetype.org/blog/tt-norms-pro-a-8-year-history-of-the-font-family/)*

The project initiated by TypeType Foundry in 2015 with the goal of creating a modern, neutral, and geometric sans serif typeface debuted with its first release in 2017, quickly attracting great interest and climbing to the top ranks on MyFonts. Over time, with the addition of expanded Latin and Cyrillic support, additional style options, and technical improvements, a more comprehensive version was published in 2019 under the name [TT Norms® Pro](https://typetype.org/fonts/tt-norms-pro/). The [article](https://typetype.org/blog/tt-norms-pro-a-8-year-history-of-the-font-family/) details the story of its transformation over the past eight years into a comprehensive and flexible type family that brings together aesthetics and functionality.

## The power of the letter g in brand identities

*Image: © [fabiohaagtype.com](https://fabiohaagtype.com/en/omgs/)*

In their [article](https://fabiohaagtype.com/en/omgs/) emphasizing that the letter *g* holds a special significance in typefaces designed for branding, Fabio Haag Type notes that people focus on reading rather than seeing typefaces, which is why a design must both maintain legibility and bring uniqueness to the brand identity. To achieve this balance, the letter g stands out as an ideal tool because possessing two distinct structures—single-story and double-story—offers designers the opportunity to create striking and legible forms.

## Empathy through letters: The emotional power of typography

*Image: © [Print](https://www.printmag.com/type-tuesday/type-as-a-model-for-empathy-inclusivity/)*

[According to Kim Tidwell](https://www.printmag.com/type-tuesday/type-as-a-model-for-empathy-inclusivity/) of Print magazine, typography can be an effective tool that fosters empathy and inclusivity. Citing examples such as the typeface [*Silent Shake*](https://www.dandad.org/awards/new-blood/2025/monotype/4630/silent-shake/), designed by University of Leeds students to contribute to diagnosis and awareness by visualizing early-stage handwriting changes in Parkinson’s disease; the [*Ripple Migraine Care*](https://www.sarahsee.com/portfolio-collections/branding/ripple-migraine-care) brand identity, which connects with patients through distorted wordmarks mimicking migraines; and the book [*4,4,4&2*](https://www.boomsaloon.com/4442-book/), which reflects the cognitive effects of dementia using disappearing ink with the contribution of an Alzheimer’s patient, Tidwell emphasizes that typography has become a medium of emotional resonance that enables people to experience the world from different perspectives and encourages empathy.

## The rise of the Live, Laugh, Love font

*Image: © [The Independent](https://www.the-independent.com/life-style/wedding-font-calligraphy-cursive-live-laugh-love-b2791515.html)*

Katie Rosseinsky [examines](https://www.the-independent.com/life-style/wedding-font-calligraphy-cursive-live-laugh-love-b2791515.html) the rise of the *Live, Laugh, Love* font, seen almost everywhere from ordinary household items to wedding and bachelorette party invitations. According to Rosseinsky, this decorative script rapidly gained popularity around 2015 via Pinterest and social media platforms. Designers, meanwhile, note that the font was used for decorative purposes rather than legibility and initially added a warm touch. However, having become a cliché over time due to overuse, this font still appears as a nostalgic and meaningful choice for special events.

## Beneventan script from the Middle Ages to digital typography

*Image: © [tiro.com](https://www.tiro.com/articles/beneventan)*

John Hudson [explores](https://www.tiro.com/articles/beneventan) the contradictory relationship between writing and forgetfulness through the *Beneventan* script, developed at the Benedictine Abbey of Monte Cassino. Drawing from the story of Theuth and Thutmose in Plato’s *Phaedrus* dialogue, he draws attention to the notion that writing, rather than strengthening memory, might make it lazy and prevent truly comprehending knowledge. In addition, the article mentions contemporary examples that revive the Beneventan script style, such as Ryoichi Tsunekawa’s “Agedage Beneventan” and Lazar Dimitrijević’s “Cal Beneventan Minuscule”.

## Sylvestre’s Alphabet Album

*Image: © [publicdomainreview.org](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/sylvestre-alphabet-album/)*

French artist Joseph-Balthazar Silvestre’s (1791–1869) 1843 [Alphabet Album](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/sylvestre-alphabet-album/) presents a wide range of alphabets, spanning from crystal-clear typefaces to forms that are aesthetically elegant yet difficult to read. Because the lettering specimens in the album, dating up to the 1840s, are compiled by century, geography, or generally by the library from which they were sourced, they provide an opportunity to trace the formal transformations undergone by Roman letters over the centuries.

## New book: “A Study on the Letterpress Journey in Turkey”

*Image: © [Paradigma Akademi](https://www.paradigmaakademiyayinlari.com/12940/tipo-baskinin-turkiye-seruveni-uzerine-bir-arastirma/)*

The book titled “[A Study on the Letterpress Journey in Turkey](https://www.paradigmaakademiyayinlari.com/12940/tipo-baskinin-turkiye-seruveni-uzerine-bir-arastirma/)”, prepared by Ayşegül İzer, Başak Ürkmez, Selen Başer Nejat, and Tuğçe İşçi, has been published by *Paradigma Akademi*. Prepared within the scope of a research project conducted at Mimar Sinan Fine Arts University, the book includes interviews with craftsmen and designers who have devoted many years to printing and graphic design, alongside a glossary section explaining technical terms.

## How to enable kerning and ligatures in Canva?

*Image: © [typeheist.co](https://typeheist.co/blog/enable-kerning-and-ligatures-in-canva/)*

Canva now [supports](https://typeheist.co/blog/enable-kerning-and-ligatures-in-canva/) kerning and ligatures. The features can be enabled from the *Typography* tab added under the *Advanced Formatting* menu. Note, however, that these features will only work if supported by the selected font.

## Monotype’s collaboration with Pencil AI

*Image: © [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com/company/press-release/pencil-drives-creativity-brands-monotype-customer-integration)*

Monotype [announced](https://www.monotype.com/company/press-release/pencil-drives-creativity-brands-monotype-customer-integration) its partnership with AI-powered advertising platform Pencil. Thanks to this collaboration, users can utilize Monotype’s typefaces directly within [Pencil](https://trypencil.com/), both boosting creativity and ensuring consistency in brand identity. While officials draw attention to the importance of typography in brand expression, they emphasize that this partnership will accelerate the content production process and enable design teams to work more efficiently.

## Steven Heller’s typeface of the month

*Image: I Love Typography © [Tana Kosiyabong’s typeface Experimo.](https://ilovetypography.com/2025/08/14/steven-hellers-font-of-the-month-experimo/)*

In I Love Typography’s [*Font of the Month*](https://ilovetypography.com/2025/08/14/steven-hellers-font-of-the-month-experimo/) column, Steven Heller selects a typeface each month and examines it in detail. This month’s featured typeface is [Experimo](https://fonts.ilovetypography.com/fonts/r9-type-design/experimo), designed by Tana Kosiyabong! Heller describes Experimo not only as a versatile type system, but also as a pictographic treasure akin to emojis, offering dozens of iconic alternatives that can be used in place of letters.

## Typography competition call from Communication Arts

*Image: © [commarts.com](https://www.commarts.com/competition/2026-typography)*

Honoring the work of talented designers from around the world for 66 years, *Communication Arts* has [announced](https://www.commarts.com/competition/2026-typography) its 2026 Typography Competition. The deadline for entries—accepting work created between September 2024 and September 2025 that was not generated by artificial intelligence—is September 12, 2025. Winning works will gain international visibility by being published in Communication Arts’ Typography Annual and on the magazine’s website. In addition, each winning work will be honored with an *Award of Excellence* and a certificate.

## Symposium of the Typographic Society of Austria

*Image: © [Symposion Raabs](https://typographischegesellschaft.at/en/raabs25)*

Organized on the occasion of the twenty-fifth anniversary of the Typographic Society of Austria (TGA), [Symposion Raabs](https://typographischegesellschaft.at/en/raabs25) was held at Raabs Castle between August 28 and 31, 2025. ## Sign painters gathered in Valencia.

*Image: © [bl.ag](https://bl.ag/letterheads-per-lhorta-an-intimate-international-meet/)*

Held this past May in Almàssera, located just outside Valencia, the [*Letterheads Per L’Horta*](https://bl.ag/letterheads-per-lhorta-an-intimate-international-meet/) event brought together around forty-five participants from eleven countries.
