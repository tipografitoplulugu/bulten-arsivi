## TFF lifts the Arial requirement.

*Image: © [TFF](https://x.com/TFF_Org/status/1950933292787576987)*

The campaign launched against the Turkish Football Federation's policy mandating the use of Arial for jersey names and numbers has yielded results. Following a decision taken at the TFF Board of Directors meeting dated July 31, 2025 (numbered 48), the [Sporting Equipment Directive](https://www.tff.org/Resources/TFF/Documents/TALIMATLAR/Sportif-Ekipman-Talimati.pdf) was amended, and the requirement to use Arial on jerseys was [lifted](https://x.com/TFF_Org/status/1950933292787576987). Consequently, football clubs will be able to use any typeface they wish on their jerseys, provided that it meets legibility criteria and receives approval from the TFF. In addition, typeface changes may also be made after the season begins, in accordance with the conditions specified in the directive.

While the new regulation grants clubs greater design freedom, it also brings along some practical uncertainties. Although Article 4 of the directive states, “It is essential that the numbers and names used be in an easily legible typeface on the back of the jersey,” the lack of clarification regarding which criteria legibility will be evaluated against and what expertise the decision-makers will hold raises questions.

As you may recall, in the [article](https://medium.com/tipografi/sahadaki-harfler-spor-markalamas%C4%B1nda-tipografinin-y%C3%BCkselen-g%C3%BCc%C3%BC-8f249d1300ad) we published in early July, we highlighted the rising power of typography in sports branding and examined examples of how clubs worldwide strengthen their identities through custom typefaces. This decision taken by the TFF can be regarded as an important step toward developing the visual identities of local clubs. However, time will tell whether this freedom will yield creative and high-quality results, as seen in international examples.

## European Design Awards announced.

*Image: © [European Design](https://awards.europeandesign.org/winners)*

The 2025 European Design Awards have been [announced](https://awards.europeandesign.org/winners). In the competition, where Onur Yazıcıgil from Turkey was among the jury members, TypeTogether from Czechia won the Gold award in the Text Typeface category with their typeface [*Playwrite*](https://fonts.googleblog.com/2024/06/playwrite-is-new-font-superfamily-for.html). Schrifteria Foundry from Serbia with [*SFT Schrifted Serif*](https://schrifteria.xyz/sft-schrifted-serif) and Neue from Germany with [*Neue Serie57*](https://www.neue.shop/fonts/neue-serie57) each won Bronze awards. In the Display Typeface category, TypeMates from Germany was awarded Gold with [*Piet*](https://www.typemates.com/fonts/piet). Kostas Bartsokas from the United Kingdom won Silver with the typeface *Canicule Display*, while Briefcase Type Foundry from Czechia took Bronze with [*BC Civitas*](https://www.briefcasetype.com/fonts/bc-civitas/about).

## Jim Parkinson passes away.

*Image: Jim Parkinson (1941–2025) © [Typographica](https://typographica.org/on-typography/jim-parkinson-1941-2025/)*

American type designer [Jim Parkinson](https://typedesign.com/About.html) [passed away](https://typographica.org/on-typography/jim-parkinson-1941-2025/) on June 26, 2025, at the age of 83. Best known for designing the iconic logo of Rolling Stone magazine, Parkinson produced dozens of works over a career spanning more than fifty years, ranging from type designs to publications such as Esquire and the Los Angeles Times, as well as musical acts like the Doobie Brothers. Parkinson, who passed away due to complications from Alzheimer’s disease, left behind an enduring typographic legacy.

## Buchstabenmuseum Berlin is closing!

*Image: © [Buchstabenmuseum](https://www.buchstabenmuseum.de/en/)*

The Buchstabenmuseum in Berlin has [announced](https://www.instagram.com/p/DMFELQboU7m/) that it is closing. Hosting more than two thousand items of vernacular lettering in its collection, the museum can be visited until October 5, 2025. In addition, the museum, which is searching for a long-term storage facility for its collection, is seeking [support](https://www.buchstabenmuseum.de/en/support/) for this matter.

## A critical look at font licensing: Font Licensing Mess

*Image: © [Font Licensing Mess](https://fontlicensingmess.com/)*

[Font Licensing Mess](https://fontlicensingmess.com/), an independent research project conducted by María Ramos and Ana Moliz, aims to guide interested parties by examining the complex and ambiguous world of font licenses through clarity, structure, and a data-driven approach. The project does not merely document the existing font licensing landscape; it also aims to make the system’s contradictions visible, encourage discussion, and generate ideas for licensing models better suited to the needs of type designers, type foundries, and their users.

## TikTok Sans is now open source!

*Image: TikTok Sans © [Print](https://www.printmag.com/design-news/type-network-expands-tiktok-sans-from-brand-typeface-to-open-source/)*

> “When you examine a typeface designed by someone else, you do a kind of forensic or archaeological work. It’s a great pleasure to explore what exists, enhance it from a new perspective, and turn it into an even more versatile type family.” Maria Doreuli

In the [bulletin](https://medium.com/tipografi/tipografi-b%C3%BClteni-aral%C4%B1k-2024-b5fc8e46b4ce) we published in December 2024, we shared [TikTok Sans](https://www.grillitype.com/commissions/tiktok-custom-font), the custom typeface Grilli Type designed for TikTok. Now, there is a significant development regarding this typeface: as a result of a collaboration led by Type Network, TikTok Sans has been made available to everyone via [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/TikTok+Sans) in an open-source and expanded version.

The typeface developed by TikTok with Grilli Type was transformed into a multi-axis type system with contributions from Type Network, Contrast Foundry, and Aaron Bell. It has now become a typeface that anyone—not just a single brand—can freely use and build upon.

## ATypI’s new president Nada Abdallah

*Image: © [ATypI](https://atypi.org/2025/07/02/nada-abdallah-elected-president-laurence-penney-vice-president/)*

In the [bulletin](https://medium.com/tipografi/tipografi-b%C3%BClteni-nisan-2024-df8f79c906db) we published in April 2024, we announced that [Nada Abdallah](https://atypi.org/about-atypi/who-we-are/elections-2023-24-atypi-board-of-directors/board-candidate-nada-abdallah/) from the United Arab Emirates was elected as ATypI’s new vice president on March 8, 2024. At the Board of Directors meeting held on June 30, 2025, Nada Abdallah was unanimously [elected](https://atypi.org/2025/07/02/nada-abdallah-elected-president-laurence-penney-vice-president/) as ATypI’s new president. At the same meeting, [Laurence Penney](https://www.lorp.org/), a long-time member of ATypI who joined the Board of Directors in 2023, was elected vice president. A member of the ATypI Board of Directors since 2023, Abdallah is renowned for her background in typographic research, education, and cross-cultural design. During her presidency, Abdallah aims to expand ATypI’s reach and inclusivity in underrepresented regions, encourage academic participation, and support diversity.

## Call for proposals opens for ATypI 2026 conferences.

*Image: © [ATypI](https://atypi.org/2025/07/23/atypi-2026-call-for-proposals-is-open)*

There is more news from ATypI: ATypI has [started accepting proposals](https://atypi.org/2025/07/23/atypi-2026-call-for-proposals-is-open) for its 2026 conferences. The conferences will be held at Stanford University between May 27–30, 2026, and at the University of Sharjah in the United Arab Emirates between October 28–31, 2026. Under the theme “Homescreen: Design for Belonging,” the Stanford conference aims to blend the historical legacy of digital typography with today’s inclusive, sustainable, and technological vision of the future. The Sharjah conference, under the theme “Between: Tradition | Transition | Transformation,” aims to examine typographic practice beyond borders in a transformative context across different cultures, writing systems, and disciplines.

## 1,600-year-old set of iron weights found in Uzuncaburç.

*Image: © [turkiyetoday.com](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/culture/1600-year-old-set-of-iron-weights-found-in-turkiyes-ancient-city-of-uzuncaburc-3203997)*

During excavations carried out in the ancient city of Uzuncaburç (Diocaesarea) in Mersin’s Silifke district, an approximately 1,600-year-old set of iron weights shaped like letters and a complete steelyard scale set were [found](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/culture/1600-year-old-set-of-iron-weights-found-in-turkiyes-ancient-city-of-uzuncaburc-3203997). The weights, each crafted in the shape of letters of the Ancient Greek alphabet, are estimated to have been used between the fourth and seventh centuries.

## Artificial intelligence and the future of typography

*Image: © [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/688637/typography-fonts-ai)*

> “The future of typography is uncertain!”

Geoffrey Bunting penned an [article](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/688637/typography-fonts-ai) discussing the potential impacts of artificial intelligence on typography, drawing from Monotype’s “[Re:Vision](https://www.monotype.com/type-trends)” report, which we featured in our [March 2025 bulletin](https://medium.com/tipografi/tipografi-b%C3%BClteni-mart-2025-09e6ff1f9a12). According to Bunting, artificial intelligence could democratize type design by opening it to broader audiences, but this could also bring ethical questions such as the loss of creative control to the fore. Noting that some experts compare these developments to the industrial transformations of the early twentieth century, Bunting anticipates that realistic applications are still limited, yet AI could make significant contributions to multilingual writing systems.

## Chopsticks as emissaries of Japanese typography and culture

*Image: © [Letterform Archive](https://letterformarchive.org/news/this-just-in-chopstick-sleeves-as-emissaries-of-japanese-typography-and-culture)*

Recently donated to Letterform Archive’s collection, [Japanese chopstick sleeves](https://letterformarchive.org/news/this-just-in-chopstick-sleeves-as-emissaries-of-japanese-typography-and-culture) dating back to the 1970s reveal the role of typography on everyday objects. The subtleties of Japanese typography, period aesthetics, and visual communication language on *hashibukuro*—the chopstick sleeves or small paper wrappers enclosing chopsticks—emerge in a remarkable way.

## Rosetta Type offers a typeface that belongs only to you!

*Image: © [*Only Yours*](https://www.creativeboom.com/resources/only-yours-a-bold-new-approach-to-typography-based-on-personalisation/)*

With their project titled *Only Yours*, [Rosetta Type](https://rosettatype.com/) offers an experimental type system that is completely bespoke, based on the concept of personalized typography. Challenging traditional licensing models, [Only Yours](https://www.creativeboom.com/resources/only-yours-a-bold-new-approach-to-typography-based-on-personalisation/) provides each user with a unique version. The form of each character is created through various combinations of defined design elements, and that exact same version is never offered to anyone else again.

## Asemic Writing Workshop

*Image: © [Asemic Writing Workshop](https://linktr.ee/memederdener)*

Memed Erdener organized an [Asemic Writing Workshop](https://linktr.ee/memederdener) on July 4, 2025. Defined as a collection of visual marks that are meaningless or undecipherable yet resemble writing, the asemic writing workshop offers a two-hour lesson on writing and line drawing.

## A workshop extending from wood type to digital typefaces: X Cicéro

*Image: © [X Cicéro](https://xcicero.esad-gv.net/)*

In the type design workshop titled [X Cicéro](https://xcicero.esad-gv.net/), offered to graphic design students at [ESAD](https://esad-gv.fr/) in Valence, France, the wood type collection used in the school’s print shop was converted into digital typefaces. Different character sets—most of which were incomplete and damaged—were cataloged, measured, and identified as much as possible; all resulting typefaces are now available for free download.

## New type foundry Plomb Type

*Image: © [Plomb Type](https://www.plombtype.com/)*

Despite the ongoing monopolization in the type industry, independent type foundries continue to be established. Founded by Max Esnée and Emma Marichal, France-based [Plomb Type](https://www.plombtype.com/) currently has one typeface in its library named [Savate](https://www.plombtype.com/savate/). While advocating for the value of type as a cultural artifact, the type foundry aims to provide a fairer and more transparent environment for designers.
