## A sans-serif for the needs of the 21st century: Zed

Typotheque type foundry announced their new typeface [Zed](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/zed-a-sans-for-the-needs-of-21century), which they describe as “a sans-serif for the needs of the twenty-first century.” The foundry states that most twentieth-century sans-serif typefaces compromise on design, whereas Zed was developed using the latest research on which letterforms are most easily read by the widest audience, thus directly addressing this shortcoming with different text and display versions. The foundry [tested](https://www.typotheque.com/articles/designing-fonts-with-low-vision-readers-in-mind) Zed with the help of visually impaired readers and worked with language communities and local designers around the world. Additionally, Zed was awarded a [Gold European Design Award](https://x.com/typotheque/status/1798994593394106378). 🇬🇧

## A new collaboration from Monotype

Monotype [joined](https://www.fastcompany.com/91135887/monotype-joins-readability-consortium) [The Readability Consortium](https://thereadabilityconsortium.org/), a research group focused on improving digital readability. Adobe and Google also support the research of this community of more than two hundred scientists from various disciplines, which aims to make the internet a more accessible place. 🇬🇧

## Revival as an experience in type design

## The history of publishing from the 20th to the 21st century

“[A History of Publishing from the 20th to the 21st Century](https://www.noktayayin.com/urun/20-yuzyildan-21-yuzyila-yayinciligin-tarihi-richard-charkin-9786259472409),” a book shedding light on the changing dynamics of book publishing over the last fifty years by Richard Charkin, former president of the International Publishers Association and senior executive at publishing houses such as Oxford University Press, Bloomsbury, and Macmillan Publishers, has been published by Nokta Yayın.

## A special book for type specimen collectors

Featuring 86 typefaces and 72 logo letterings designed by Fernando Mello between 2001 and 2023, the 432-page “[Numune N°0: Desenho Tipográfico](https://www.instagram.com/p/C3z-QjZrqNn/)” is available only upon request as it was printed in a limited edition. Those interested in type specimens can also check out the 1819 specimen in the St Bride Library archives, which contains William Caslon IV’s (1781–1869) typeface [Two-Lines English Egyptian](https://archive.org/details/6027_20211124/page/n27/mode/2up), first sold commercially in 1816.

## TypeType’s typefaces are on Adobe Fonts!

Several typefaces from [TypeType type foundry](https://typetype.org/), founded in 2013 by Ivan Gladkikh—such as *TT Autonomous*, *TT Commons Pro*, and *TT Globs*—have been added to the [Adobe Fonts](https://fonts.adobe.com/foundries/typetype) library.

## The typeface bridging design and programming: Krata

Created by [Remi Forte](https://rmfrt.com/), the display typeface [Krata](https://www.205.tf/krata) [merges](https://www.printmag.com/type-tuesday/krata-typeface-remi-forte/) the iterative nature of design with the logic of programming. At the core of Krata’s creative constraints is a Python program that applies horizontal or vertical line patterns to characters using a grid framework. 🇬🇧

## Yahşibey Design Workshops

A workshop on type design inspired by the architecture of the ancient cities of Bergama and Aigai will be held at [Yahşibey Design Workshops](http://yahsiworkshops.com/). The workshop, which will take place between August 1–15, 2024, will be led by Ayşegül Özer, Selen Başer Nejat, and Tuğçe İşçi Özen.

## A humble typeface: Austerlitz

An article on the design process of [Austerlitz](https://typofonderie.com/gazette/austerlitz-a-humble-typeface), the new typeface designed by Typofonderie type foundry, inspired by Série 16 published around 1878. As the foundry’s fourth modern (Didone) typeface—following Ambroise, Mencken Head, and AW Conqueror Didot—Austerlitz features four optical sizes and 56 fonts, including italics. 🇬🇧

## What is realness? Freeing type from a fixed viewpoint

Starting from the question, “Can a single typeface express multiple viewpoints through its functionality?”, Ben Kiel, Jesse Ragan, Silas Munro, and Brian Johnson [discuss](https://coopertype.org/events/what-is-realness-freeing-type-from-a-fixed-viewpoint) their collaboration translating broad notions of personal identity into typographic form. The presentation took place on June 10, 2024, in the Rose Auditorium at Cooper Union as part of [Type@Cooper](https://coopertype.org/)’s Herb Lubalin Lecture Series. 🇬🇧
