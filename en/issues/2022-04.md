# Typography Newsletter: April 2022

### Monthly summary of notable developments in the world of typography

⏰ **Reading Time:** ~7 minutes

## Research on brass type

[Dan Reynolds](https://twitter.com/typeoff) has started [research](https://www.typeoff.de/2022/03/germanys-brass-type-foundries-project-start/) on brass type. In his research, Reynolds focuses on brass type produced in the 1880s for poster printing and bookbinders through the history of type foundries that used brass in type production in Germany, particularly companies such as Maas & Jungvogel in Krefeld. 🇬🇧

## New book from Counter-Print: “Big Type”

Examining typography-focused graphic design and visual identity work, “[Big Type](https://www.counter-print.co.uk/collections/all-books/products/big-type)” has taken its place among the typography books published in 2022. Published by [Counter-Print](https://www.counter-print.co.uk) and filled with work from the world’s top design studios, “Big Type” also includes interviews and detailed project descriptions. 🇬🇧

Monotype has published its 2022 [type trends report](https://www.monotype.com/type-trends). The report features insights into how design reflects society, key trends in typography and branding, and how the creative world is embracing technologies such as animation, variable fonts, and NFTs. 🇬🇧

An [article worth reading](https://forgotten-shapes.com/monument?article=monument-and-consul-2) about two sans serif typefaces released in Berlin in 1903: Monument from Emil Gursch’s type foundry and Consul from Wilhelm Woellmer’s type foundry. 🇬🇧

## Bringing Canal Brasil to life with a variable font

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://vimeo.com/465228890)*

Type Network partner [Plau Design](https://www.plau.design/), together with Brazilian advertising agency [Tátil Design](https://tatil.com.br/en/tatil-home-en/), refreshed the visual identity of [Canal Brasil](https://canaisglobo.globo.com/c/canal-brasil/). A [custom variable font](https://custom.typenetwork.com/case_studies/custom-variable-font-canal-brasil) was designed for the refreshed visual identity of the TV channel, which was founded in 1998 and holds an established place in the minds of Brazilians. The new visual identity won two gold awards and the Grand Prix at the [Brasil Design Award](https://brasildesignaward.com.br/). 🇬🇧

## The TDC awards have found their winners.

The winners of the type and communication design competitions organized by the [Type Directors Club](https://www.tdc.org), with designers from more than sixty countries participating across professional and student categories, [have been announced](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/tdcawards/). In the Poster category of the Communication Design competition, the work titled “[Stones Do Remember.](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/tdcawards/-award/41959/stones-do-remember)” by [Erman Yılmaz](http://ermanyilmaz.com) from our country was awarded. In addition, the TDC announced a portfolio-based competition called [Ascenders](http://ascenders.tdc.org), welcoming inspiring work dedicated to typography in all its versatile forms from designers under thirty-five. The early deadline for the competition is April 30, and the final deadline is May 31, 2022.

## A book on the unstoppable rise of sans serif: “SANS (FR) 1628–1924”

Focusing on the unstoppable rise of sans serif, which was frequently used in France between the Age of Enlightenment and the early twentieth century, the book “[SANS (FR) 1628–1924](https://editions-non-standard.com/books/sans-fr-1628-1924?lang=en)” was published by [Éditions Non Standard](https://editions-non-standard.com/?lang=en). Authored by Sébastien Morlighem, the book is priced at 35 euros. The book is also competing for the [Fedrigoni Award](https://fedrigonitopaward.com/project/non-standard-sans-fr-1628-1924/). 🇬🇧 🇫🇷

## Think you know Afrikan fonts?

In his [article](https://uxdesign.cc/you-know-afrikan-fonts-think-again-6b5bb0018d27), type designer [Tapiwanashe S. Garikayi](https://twitter.com/Sebastiangary1) prompts the reader to reconsider, pointing out that when people think of African fonts, tribal-looking, grunge, handwritten, and similar styles come to mind, and that naming typefaces inspired by various elements of African culture as *Afrikan Fonts, Afrikan Typefaces*, or *Afrikan Typography* is misguided. 🇬🇧

## Decolonizing typography: A type design for African writing systems

[Tapiwanashe S. Garikayi](https://twitter.com/Sebastiangary1) [shared](https://uxdesign.cc/bassa-vah-a-type-design-case-study-a1ed4c4307a6) the design process of the typeface he created for the [Bassa Vah script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassa_Vah_script)—used by the Bassa people of roughly six hundred thousand in Liberia and Sierra Leone, and developed in the late 1800s by Thomas Flo Lewis inspired by traditional Bassa symbols and motifs. 🇬🇧

## Open-source tool for proper typesetting on the web: Mechanical Ragger

[Oak Studios](https://oak.is) has released an open-source tool called [Mechanical Ragger](https://oak.is/thinking/mechanical-ragger/) that prevents uneven lines on web pages. Optically balancing the uneven spaces—referred to in English as the “rag”—created on the right side of left-aligned lines in Latin typography, the tool offers better readability, especially in long-form text. 🇬🇧

## An exhibition held in memory of Ange Degheest

Type design workshop students from [EESAB](https://www.eesab.fr), an art academy in the Brittany region of France, along with their instructors, organized an [exhibition](https://velvetyne.fr/degheest/infos.html) in memory of French type designer Ange Degheest (1928–2009) as part of the *Une saison graphique 2020* festival. The [exhibition](https://velvetyne.fr/degheest/infos.html) features original objects and documents from personal archives opened to the public for the first time concerning the designer, who has been largely forgotten and bore witness to the technical upheavals in printing and telecommunications during the second half of the twentieth century. In addition, as part of the exhibition, Poem Editions published a twenty-four-page [booklet](https://www.poem-editions.com/products/degheest) that includes an essay titled “The female ghost of French type history” by [Alice Savoie](https://twitter.com/alicesavoie?utm_source=aposto), whose project [Women in Type](https://www.women-in-type.com/) we covered extensively in January’s [Typography Bulletin](https://apos.to/s/61bf2de91486a5000641a573). Degheest’s type designs were revived and made available under an open font license through the [Velvetyne Type Foundry](http://velvetyne.fr/fonts/degheest/) website. 🇬🇧

## Typographic roots of the Dutch company Océ

An [article](http://dutchdesigndaily.com/complete-overview/early-development-digital-fonts-oce-research-development/) by [Henk Lamers](https://twitter.com/HenkLamers) and [Jeanne de Bont](https://twitter.com/JeanneDeBont) exploring the typographic roots of copying machine manufacturer [Océ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Production_Printing), based in Venlo, the Netherlands. 🇬🇧 With roots dating back to 1877 and printing-related operations beginning in 1919, the company was acquired by Canon in 2010.

## Fifteen questions from Dinamo to type foundries

After nearly a decade of operational experience, Swiss-based award-winning type foundry [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com/studio) asked questions to fifteen type foundries—who are both competitors and friends, including Blaze Type, Klim Type Foundry, Commercial Type, and OH no Type Company—on topics such as the lifecycle of typefaces and what it means for type foundries to be acquired by monopolies. The responses make for a [good read](https://abcdinamo.com/news/type-foundry-survey-1) to learn what type foundries think about their future and the products they present to the world. 🇬🇧

## Devrim Yarası: Sociology of Trauma, Alphabet Reform, and Collective Memory

With his book “[Devrim Yarası](https://www.academia.edu/50886307/Devrim_Yaras%C4%B1_Travma_Sosyolojisi_Harf_%C4%B0nk%C4%B1l%C3%A2b%C4%B1_ve_Toplumsal_Haf%C4%B1za),” Dr. Ekrem Saltık addresses the founding process of the Republic of Turkey—which experienced a traumatic eclipse of the past—from the perspective of the *sociology of trauma*, through the lens of the Alphabet Reform that took place nearly a century ago. The [interview](https://youtu.be/s1FGMEEFf6U) Saltık attended regarding his book can be watched here.

## New book on wood type from David Shields

[David Shields](https://twitter.com/19cWT)’s [book](https://utpress.utexas.edu/books/shields-rob-roy-kelly-american-wood-type-collection) compiling the collection of wood type produced and used in printing in nineteenth-century America by American design educator and historian [Rob Roy Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Roy_Kelly) will be published soon. Kelly, who also authored a book in 1969 on wood type and nineteenth-century typography history, assembled the collection starting in 1957; currently managed by the University of Texas, it comprises approximately one hundred and fifty typefaces in various sizes and styles.

## “It all starts with writing.”

An [article](https://www.typemag.org/post/it-all-starts-with-writing-gerrit-noordzij) by Jan Middendorp on Gerrit Noordzij (1931–2022), who recently passed away. 🇬🇧 One of the most remarkable figures among those who shaped contemporary typographic culture—large-framed, energetic, possessing even at age 87 the sharp eyes of a scholar seeking to make sense of many things, and renowned for his 1985 book “The Stroke: Theory of Writing”—Noordzij’s influence extends from type design to the outer reaches of graphic design.

## Unicode ends flag emoji proposals.

With Emoji 16.0, the Unicode Consortium [will no longer accept](https://home.unicode.org/the-past-and-future-of-flag-emoji/) flag emoji proposals as of July 31, 2022. 🇬🇧

## A font mimicking the reading experience of someone with dyslexia

Illustrator [Rocio Egio](https://www.instagram.com/rocioegio/) and designer [Pranav Bhardwaj](https://www.instagram.com/pranavxbhardwaj/) came together to create a font that mimics the reading experience of someone with dyslexia. Inspired by the illustration Egio designed on the occasion of World Dyslexia Day based on her own experience, Bhardwaj [designed a custom typeface](https://www.stirworld.com/see-features-a-typeface-that-captures-the-dyslexic-reading-experience?mc_cid=0a79af8b07&mc_eid=48a95a3cf1) called *Dyslexic Font*. 🇬🇧

## Symbols using variable font technology

[Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com) announced a [symbol set](https://fonts.google.com/icons) utilizing variable font technology. Containing over two thousand five hundred glyphs in a single file, the variable font offers four axes across three different styles: fill, weight, grade, and optical size. 🇬🇧

## On Ukraine’s type design heritage

[Katerina Korolevtseva](https://korolevtseva.com) [wrote](https://www.alphabettes.org/ukrainian-type-design-heritage-in-modern-context/) about Ukraine’s type design heritage. Inspired by a book cover designed in 1954 by [Mykhailo Dmytrenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mychajlo_Dmytrenko) (1908–1997), Korolevtseva aims to bridge the past and present by considering historical examples in a modern context. 🇬🇧

## A project documenting Madrid’s old signs: Paco Graco

A project that documents, rescues, and preserves Madrid’s old signs: [Paco Graco](https://pacograco.org/mapa/). 🇪🇸 Aiming to defend and protect Madrid’s shared heritage, which has recently come under serious threat, the project presents identified signs to users via an interactive map. In the December 2021 issue of our [Typography Bulletin](https://apos.to/i/tipografi-bulteni-aralik-2021), we touched upon the growing interest in vernacular typography both worldwide and in Turkey.
