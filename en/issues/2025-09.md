# Typography Newsletter: September 2025

### In this month’s bulletin, there are significant developments in typography from around the world and Turkey.

⏰ **Reading Time:** ~7 minutes

Hello everyone!

Highlights, new typefaces, and developments from the world of typography in the September 2025 bulletin of the Turkish Typography Society:

---

## New type family from Oğuzhan Cengiz: Arketip

*Image: © [Arketip](https://www.instagram.com/p/DOJ10G2gJGM/)*

[Oğuzhan Cengiz](https://www.myfonts.com/collections/hurufatfont-type-foundry) announced his new sans serif type family, [Arketip](https://www.instagram.com/p/DOJ10G2gJGM/). Featuring sixteen weights across nine widths, Arketip is a true superfamily with 288 styles.

---

## New typeface from Fatih Hardal: FH Dfaalt

*Image: © [FH Dfaalt](https://www.typografische.com/typefaces/fh-dfaalt)*

[Fatih Hardal](https://www.typografische.com/) announced his new typeface, [FH Dfaalt](https://www.typografische.com/typefaces/fh-dfaalt), inspired by prominent representatives of the grotesque sans serif tradition. Steering clear of exaggerated formal features and emphasizing functionality, simplicity, and versatility, the typeface offers six weights along with italics.

---

## New type family for The Big Issue magazine

*Image: © [TYPE01](https://type-01.com/typography-for-social-change-the-story-behind-the-big-issue-type-family/)*

For the thirtieth anniversary of [The Big Issue](https://www.bigissue.com/) magazine—founded in 1991 to reintegrate people experiencing homelessness into society by providing them with an income—a type family called [*Big Issue*](https://blast-foundry.com/typefaces/big-issue) was [designed](https://type-01.com/typography-for-social-change-the-story-behind-the-big-issue-type-family/) by [Blast Foundry](https://blast-foundry.com/) in a design process led by Pentagram designer Matt Willey. Designed for versatility and striking headlines, this family features two subfamilies, Display and Text, offering various weights from Light to Black along with italic styles. In addition, a portion of the proceeds from this publicly available type family is donated to support *The Big Issue* project, aiming to create a positive impact beyond design.

## Tausend: An innovative approach to the geometric grotesque tradition

*Image: © [Tausend](https://fontwerk.com/en/text/tausend)*

The [article](https://fontwerk.com/en/text/tausend) on the design process of [Tausend](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/tausend-collection), a new sans serif type collection designed by Christoph Koeberlin and Gabriel Richter, explores the collection’s starting point and development stages. The story begins with a characteristic letter “a” that emerged during Koeberlin’s design of [Tresor](https://www.nan.xyz/fonts/nan-tresor/). With type designer [Gabriel Richter](https://fontwerk.com/en/designers/gabriel-richter) joining the project—where the structural features of this letter formed the collection’s DNA—modernity, dynamism, and a fresh interpretation of the geometric grotesque tradition are added. Tausend is described as a powerful family not only aesthetically, but also functionally. Its support for the DIN 91379 standard, which meets the official requirements of many languages including EU countries, makes it a reliable choice for institutions and brands. While the type family offers a wide range of styles with different typefaces such as Plakat, Soft, Stencil, and Shaded, adaptations provided in optical sizes promise high legibility from small text to large headlines.

## Futura’s new multiscript adaptation: Futura®100

*Image: © [TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com/new-release-futura100)*

Developed in collaboration between TypeTogether and Bauer Types, [Futura®100](https://www.type-together.com/futura100-multiscript), a multiscript adaptation of classic Futura renowned for its minimalist geometry, was [introduced](https://www.type-together.com/new-release-futura100). Enhancing legibility with italics, variable font options, and optical size support, Futura®100 currently covers 12 languages. In 2026, it will expand with 11 additional scripts to become a universal type family.

## New publication from Dinamo: “Dinamo Specimen 2”

*Image: © [Dinamo](https://www.instagram.com/p/DPOwbCJClga/?igsh=dnE3dTVnbGtzcTJi)*

Dinamo Type Foundry will present its new publication, “[Dinamo Specimen 2](https://www.instagram.com/p/DPOwbCJClga/?igsh=dnE3dTVnbGtzcTJi),” developed in collaboration with [HanLi](https://hanli.eu/) studio, on October 11, 2025, in Paris. This publication includes Dinamo’s existing typefaces as well as previously unreleased designs, and has the distinction of being the first publication designed in the “DIN-AMO” format.

## New research on generative neural networks in typography

*Image: © [lttrface.com](https://lttrface.com/doctoral-thesis)*

The [doctoral research](https://lttrface.com/doctoral-thesis) by Filip Paldia examines the potential of AI-based generative neural networks in the field of typography. By highlighting the limitations of methods used in type design, Paldia proposes new datasets, representations, and tools that can overcome these constraints. The study offers contributions on both theoretical and practical levels by systematically classifying existing AI-based font projects in the literature. It also highlights the creative potential of artificial intelligence in typography through curated font datasets and sample use cases.

## Typography in interface design

*Image: © [arielsalminen.com](https://arielsalminen.com/2016/typography-for-user-interfaces/)*

In his [article](https://arielsalminen.com/2016/typography-for-user-interfaces/) written in 2016, Ariel Salminen explains the role of typography in interface design, legibility, and the principles of making good typographic choices. Emphasizing that text is in fact the fundamental component of user interfaces, the article addresses both the functional and aesthetic aspects of typography. Characteristics such as easily distinguishable letters, legibility across different screen sizes and resolutions, adequate spacing, low contrast, and a large x-height are listed as essentials of a good typeface for interface design.

## On the naming of typefaces

*Image: © [shinntype.com](https://shinntype.com/on-the-naming-of-fonts/)*

Nick Shinn [recounts](https://shinntype.com/on-the-naming-of-fonts/) the process of naming typefaces through personal experiences and anecdotes. He emphasizes that the names of typefaces are determined sometimes by chance, and sometimes through deliberate choices. For example, he used his own name for the typefaces *Shinn Sans* and *Shinn Gothic*, took the name *Beaufort* from Beaufort Street in Toronto, while the name *Pratt Nova* was inspired by David Pratt, the art director of *The Globe and Mail* newspaper.

## The cultural transformation of the internet through font hacks

*Image: © [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com/news/michelle-santiago-cortes-font-hacks-letterlike)*

Michelle Santiago Cortés [examines](https://abcdinamo.com/news/michelle-santiago-cortes-font-hacks-letterlike) “font hack” practices ranging from ASCII to Unicode. The article shows how early internet aesthetics have permeated the present day and how these hacked characters can convey nuances that simple letters cannot.

## When brand typefaces go open source

*Image: © [Google Design](https://design.google/library/open-source-custom-fonts-google)*

According to [an article by Sarah Daily](https://design.google/library/open-source-custom-fonts-google) from Google Design, many organizations are sharing custom typefaces developed for their brands as open source. Typefaces such as IBM Plex, Mona Sans, and Atkinson Hyperlegible not only contribute to brand identity, but also provide solutions to design challenges like legibility, accessibility, and multilingual use. Open source licensed typefaces eliminate licensing costs while enabling contributions from independent designers. Thus, typography becomes a shared design language that both strengthens the identity of organizations and can be shared on a global scale.

## On desktop font licenses

*Image: © [typeheist.co](https://typeheist.co/blog/choosing-the-right-desktop-license/)*

In her [article](https://typeheist.co/blog/choosing-the-right-desktop-license/) drawing attention to the importance of choosing the right desktop font license, Laura Eddy emphasizes that typefaces are not merely aesthetic choices, but design tools directly linked to copyright. Eddy provides a roadmap for font licensing, especially for desktop use, to avoid both legal issues and ethical controversies that could harm brand credibility.

## Why did Spotify hire a typographer?

*Image: © [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/fonts-typography/why-spotify-hired-a-dedicated-typographer)*

In her [article](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/fonts-typography/why-spotify-hired-a-dedicated-typographer) written for Creative Bloq, Rosie Hilder explores the reasons why Spotify hired a typographer. As reported in the piece, Spotify recognizes that within interface design and its design system *Encore*, typography is no longer a *side task*, but a standalone discipline that requires continuous maintenance and expertise. In the article, [Bianca Berning](https://bberning.com/), who works at Spotify, notes that typography is critical not only visually, but also to ensure that text reaches the user accurately, legibly, and consistently across different languages, cultures, and platforms. Hilder also points out that while artificial intelligence provides technical support in typography, this work cannot be fully automated, and human experience and oversight remain indispensable.

## A previously unknown ancient language discovered in Turkey.

*Image: © [Gazete Oksijen](https://gazeteoksijen.com/o2/turkiyede-daha-once-bilinmeyen-antik-bir-dil-kesfedildi-252630)*

Clay tablets found during excavations at the ancient Hittite capital of Hattusa in Çorum have [brought to light](https://gazeteoksijen.com/o2/turkiyede-daha-once-bilinmeyen-antik-bir-dil-kesfedildi-252630) an ancient language unknown until now. Named “Kalasmaic,” this language is believed to belong to the Kalašma people who lived approximately three thousand years ago. While the discovery holds the potential to fill gaps in Bronze Age Near Eastern history, researchers predict that the number of languages identified on the tablets—currently standing at five—could reach into the thirties.

## The evolution of writing from notches to alphabets

*Image: © [Mustafa B. Bozkurt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wHv4uxzjyHo)*

In a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wHv4uxzjyHo) published on his YouTube channel, Mustafa B. Bozkurt traces the evolution of writing through a chronological journey. The video examines how writing evolved from simple memory aids such as early notches and pictograms into more complex systems through logograms and phoneticization. By also recounting the story of new writing systems that emerged in different geographies, it summarizes the journey of writing up to the present day.

## Proposal for four new letters for the Turkish alphabet

*Image: © [Haber3](https://www.haber3.com/guncel/29-harflik-alfabe-yetersiz-deyip-4-yeni-harf-onerdi-haberi-6237544)*

Murat Ertaş, President of the Erzurum Branch of the Turkish Language and Literature Association, [advocates](https://www.haber3.com/guncel/29-harflik-alfabe-yetersiz-deyip-4-yeni-harf-onerdi-haberi-6237544) for adding four new letters to the alphabet, arguing that the current 29-letter Turkish alphabet falls short of reflecting the phonetic and lexical richness of our language. According to Ertaş, the alphabet should expand to 33 letters with these additions, and local or ancient words forgotten over time should be *revived*.

## New book: “The Quick Classification Guide for Typography”

*Image: © [niggli.ch](https://www.niggli.ch/produkt/the-quick-classification-guide-for-typography-d-2/)*

Eva Kubinyi’s book “[The Quick Classification Guide for Typography](https://www.niggli.ch/produkt/the-quick-classification-guide-for-typography-d-2/)” offers a comprehensive reference for typography beginners and experienced designers alike. Supported by 400 pages and over 250 illustrations, this book introduces the fundamental principles of typographic forms by addressing the formal, practical, and historical aspects of typefaces. Kubinyi presents a classification system so readers can recognize and use typefaces more consciously, explaining the aesthetic and cultural context behind each type family.

## New book: “The Letters of Sakarya”

*Image: © “[The Letters of Sakarya: In Pursuit of Commercial Artists and Sign Paintings](https://www.kitapyurdu.com/kitap/sakaryanin-harfleri-ticari-ressamlarin-ve-firca-yazilarinin-izinde/732750.html&filter_name=sakaryan%C4%B1n+harfleri)”*

The book “[The Letters of Sakarya: In Pursuit of Commercial Artists and Sign Paintings](https://www.kitapyurdu.com/kitap/sakaryanin-harfleri-ticari-ressamlarin-ve-firca-yazilarinin-izinde/732750.html&filter_name=sakaryan%C4%B1n+harfleri)” by one of our editors, [Murat Ertürk](https://www.muraterturk.com/), has been published by Değişim Yayınları with the support of the [Sakarya Chamber of Commerce and Industry](https://www.satso.org.tr/). The product of seven years of research, the book documents the forgotten legacy of commercial artists active in Sakarya from the 1940s to the 1990s through more than 650 photographs and archival documents. Featuring information and documents compiled from oral interviews conducted with first-degree relatives and witnesses, the first section of the book sheds light on commercial artists and the intergenerational transmission of the local lettering and illustration tradition extending into design and advertising. The second section, featuring photographic contributions by Ali Yıldırım, showcases the city’s typographic richness across six categories, ranging from title plates and industrial zone signs to shop signs and warning plaques.
