## “Can color fonts unlock a new level of expression for web typography?”

*Image: © [demagsign.io](https://demagsign.io)*

In her [article](https://demagsign.io/will-color-fonts-unlock-a-new-level-of-expression-for-web-typography/), Bianca Berning questions whether color fonts can bring a breath of fresh air to web typography. ## A database of sans-serif types sold in German-speaking countries in the 19th century

*Image: © [typeoff.de](https://www.typeoff.de/database-of-sans-serifs/)*

[Dan Reynolds](https://twitter.com/typeoff) has prepared a [database](https://www.typeoff.de/database-of-sans-serifs/) of sans-serif typefaces sold in German-speaking countries in Europe during the nineteenth century. Started by Reynolds in 2018, the project covers more than 350 specimens offered by type foundries in the region spanning Austria, Bohemia, Germany, and Switzerland—encompassing present-day Austria, Czechia, Germany, and Switzerland, as well as Gdańsk in Poland and Kaliningrad in Russia.

## A good article on ink traps

*Image: © [tosche.net](https://tosche.net/blog/ink-traps-and-pals)*

> “In general, the purpose of an ink trap is to improve the inner corners of printed type. You can see them in typefaces and logos prepared for print. It is a solution conceived for ink spots that poor printing conditions can cause in large areas of letters (such as stroke intersections). To minimize these spots, you shave off the mass in the problematic section. This technique is called an ink trap.”

An informative [article](https://tosche.net/blog/ink-traps-and-pals) about *ink traps* ([ink trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ink_trap)) from award-winning type designer [Toshi Omagari](https://tosche.net/fonts). 🇬🇧
