## Google announced 2021 search trends.

*Image: © [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/trends/)*

Google, which announces the search trends of the year every December, shared its 2021 trends. In global and country-based search trends, interest in typography can be explored by time, location, and popularity. When [looking at our country](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?hl=tr&tz=-180&geo=TR&q=tipografi&sni=3) in search trends across all categories, there appear to be two results related to the word typography: as a search term and as a visual art form. It is observed that the word *typography* was searched frequently in February–March and October–November, with Erzurum, Konya, and Denizli leading the provinces with the highest search volume; looking at the *Visual Arts Form* option, the highest number of searches took place in January, December, and April, with Trabzon, Edirne, and Bilecik leading the provinces.

## East Germany’s only type foundry: Typoart

*Image: © [Typography Guru](https://typography.guru/journal/pictures-from-the-east-german-type-foundry-typoart-r50/)*

[Photographs](https://typography.guru/journal/pictures-from-the-east-german-type-foundry-typoart-r50/) showing the production process in the 1970s and 1980s of [Typoart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VEB_Typoart), East Germany’s official type foundry that operated between 1948 and 1989. Located in the city of Dresden, East Germany’s only type foundry, Typoart, was established through the nationalization of type foundries such as *Schelter & Giesecke*, *Schriftguss*, and *Ludwig Wagner*. The type foundry’s art direction was held by [Herbert Thannhäuser](http://luc.devroye.org/fonts-24777.html) until 1963 and by [Albert Kapr](https://www.linotype.com/fr/7311/albert-kapr.html) until 1987.

## The versatile design of the lowercase sharp S (ß) glyph

*Image: The Versatile Design of the Lowercase Sharp S (ß) Glyph. © [Typography Guru](https://typography.guru/journal/german-sharp-s-design/)*

An [article has been penned](https://typography.guru/journal/german-sharp-s-design/) on the versatile design of the sharp S (ß) glyph, whose usability and design have been widely debated in recent years and which the Council for German Orthography decided some time ago should also be included in the alphabet as a capital letter. In his article, Herrmann explains the different design models of sharp S glyphs, where they come from, where they are appropriate, and how they are designed. 🇬🇧

## An environmentally conscious type family from Christoph Koeberlin: Pangea

*Image: © [Fontwerk](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/pangea-superfamily)*

[Christoph Koeberlin](https://christoph.koe.berlin) donates 25% of the proceeds from [Pangea](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/pangea-superfamily) to international organizations that sustainably reforest rainforests and work in harmony with local populations, such as [Green Belt Movement](http://www.greenbeltmovement.org/), [Trees for the Future](https://trees.org/), and [Fairventures](https://fairventures.org/). Pangea is a superfamily consisting of five weights along with italics across Latin, Cyrillic, and Greek scripts, divided into [Pangea](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/pangea) and [Pangea Text](https://fontwerk.com/en/fonts/pangea-text).

## Mentorship platform from Type Crit Crew

*Image: © [Type Crit Crew](https://typecritcrew.com)*

[Type Crit Crew](https://typecritcrew.com) connects type design students seeking consultation or critique with experienced type designers. With this initiative, Type Crit Crew aims to make type designers more accessible and approachable for students of all levels from all over the world who are eager to improve their skills, make the field more inclusive and diverse, and spread the love and passion for type. Reaching out to the volunteer type designers of Type Crit Crew is free, although some designers may request a small donation to their business or a charity of their choice.

## UNESCO added calligraphy to the “Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity” list.

*Image: © Jassim Meraj, [Art News](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/unesco-arabic-calligraphy-1234613597/)*

UNESCO [added](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/unesco-arabic-calligraphy-1234613597/) calligraphy to the “Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity” list. In its statement, UNESCO defined calligraphy as “the artistic practice of handwriting Arabic script in a fluid manner to convey harmony, grace and beauty.” The proposal was submitted by a coalition of sixteen Arabic-speaking countries, including Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Jordan, and Palestine. 🇬🇧
