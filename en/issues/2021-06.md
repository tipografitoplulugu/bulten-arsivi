## The design process of the Irregardless typeface

James Edmondson, founder of the [OH no Type ](https://ohnotype.co)type foundry, published [an article](https://ohnotype.co/blog/the-process-of-irregardless) on the design process of their recently announced new typeface, [Irregardless](https://ohnotype.co/fonts/irregardless). In the article, Edmondson states that he viewed the Irregardless typeface as an exercise in fighting his impulses and drawing habits to create something new and uncomfortable. 🇬🇧

OH no Type took [a different promotional approach](https://twitter.com/OHnoTypeCo/status/1394652391778131969) for Irregardless; for the launch, they used a recording of a phone call in which Edmondson consulted his father about the name he gave to their new typeface.

## New book from Sinan Niyazioğlu: “Alphabet and the Press”

Sinan Niyazioğlu’s book titled [Alphabet and the Press: Alphabet Reform and Press Regime in Turkey 1928–1939](https://vekam.ku.edu.tr/vekam/yayinlar/tum-yayinlar/alfabe/) has been published by [VEKAM](https://vekam.ku.edu.tr). The book examines the ten-year policy in Early Republican Turkey of materializing and instilling the discourse of nation-building into the masses through a wide range of printed materials, from alphabet primers to official propaganda publications.

## New book from TypoArabic: “Arabic Typography”

The [TypoArabic](https://twitter.com/typo_arabic) project [announced](https://research.reading.ac.uk/typoarabic/taking-stock-and-looking-ahead/) that they will be publishing a new book on Arabic typography in the coming months. Authored by [Titus Nemeth](https://twitter.com/tntype), the book—currently in draft form—will include contributions from Emanuela Conidi, Borna Izadpanah, and Gerry Leonidas, as well as [Onur Yazıcıgil](http://www.onuryazicigil.com) from our country. The book will consist of two parts: scholarly articles on historical aspects of Arabic typography and practical design considerations for current practitioners.

[TypoArabic](https://research.reading.ac.uk/typoarabic/introducing-typoarabic/) is a three-year research project funded by the European Commission under the [Horizon 2020](https://ec.europa.eu/research/mariecurieactions/msca-actions_en) program at the [Department of Typography & Graphic Communication at the University of Reading](http://www.reading.ac.uk/typography/typ-homepage.aspx).

## New type specimen from Typotheque: A world poem in 162 languages

[Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/new_monumental_typotheque_type_specimen_in_162_languages) has published [a 560-page book](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/new_monumental_typotheque_type_specimen_in_162_languages) featuring type specimens. Prepared after more than a year of work, the book includes 2,928 fonts in 162 languages. In order to present the world languages Typotheque works with—focusing on meaning as much as form—poet [Tishani Doshi](http://www.tishanidoshi.com/) was invited to write a poem addressing global issues that is at the same time deeply personal and full of hope. Embracing the given constraints, Tishani wrote the poem with one word per page. [To purchase the book…](https://www.typotheque.com/books/world_poem)

## The superpower of variable fonts: Optical size

[Roel Nieskens](https://twitter.com/PixelAmbacht) has written [a great article](https://pixelambacht.nl/2021/optical-size-hidden-superpower/) about optical size, which he describes as the superpower of variable fonts. 🇬🇧 With a variable font that has an optical size axis, glyphs can automatically adapt their appearance to suit the screen on which they are displayed.

## 72-hour TypeLab event from Typographics

The typography-focused [Typographics Design Festival](https://2021.typographics.com), which began in 2015, hosted a free 72-hour online event called [TypeLab](https://2021.typographics.com/typelab#1319), coordinated by [Petr van Blokland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_van_Blokland). Much like [the original TypeLab events held in the 1990s](http://2015.typographics.com/blog/typelab-at-typographics), the event brought together participants from around the world discussing type and design, and was broadcast live across three channels. The Typographics Design Festival is held under the coordination of [Type@Cooper](http://coopertype.org/) and [The Herb Lubalin Study Center](http://lubalincenter.cooper.edu/) at [The Cooper Union](http://www.cooper.edu/) in New York. 🇬🇧

## Thomas Gravemaker documentary

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://youtu.be/RmIVEnrhURI)*

A [short documentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RmIVEnrhURI) produced about Dutch typographer Thomas Gravemaker. Having started printing in 1970, Thomas Gravemaker worked for many years with various publishers in the UK—including several years as a senior designer at Thames & Hudson—and as a studio manager in a design group in France before founding his own workshop. His passion for letterpress began before his studies at the [Gerrit Rietveld Academie](https://rietveldacademie.nl) in Amsterdam and developed over the years. During these years, he worked on private commissions, taught letterpress, and lectured at various conferences and events.

## ILT launches its font store for users.

The typography blog [I Love Typography (ILT)](https://fonts.ilovetypography.com), founded by [John Boardley](https://twitter.com/johnboardley), introduced its new ecosystem combining font discovery and licensing with an innovative e-commerce platform. Launching a store with 7,000 fonts from 40 type foundries, ILT also makes it possible to search for fonts based on formal qualities—from general characteristics like contrast, rhythm, energy, axis, and structure down to details like serif shapes—using its font description system called [CEDARS+](https://fonts.ilovetypography.com/cedars).

Following Monotype’s major move toward monopolization by acquiring FontShop in 2014, new and alternative sales channels such as [Fontstand](https://fontstand.com), [Future Fonts](https://www.futurefonts.xyz), and [Type Network](https://www.typenetwork.com) had emerged. This move by ILT also stands as one of the most significant developments in recent times. On the other hand, [criticism](https://twitter.com/amsterdam_type/status/1409607042168410118) has begun to emerge over whether a blog that writes about fonts—even if not considered reviews or critiques—selling fonts would create a conflict of interest.

Readers who wish to do further reading can check out the in-depth article on [CEDARS+](https://fonts.ilovetypography.com/cedars), developed in collaboration with [Nadine Chahine](https://twitter.com/arabictype), [here](https://ilovetypography.com/2021/06/28/talking-about-type-introducing-cedars/), and Steven Heller’s interview with Nadine Chahine [here](https://www.printmag.com/post/the-daily-heller-i-love-you-love-we-love-typography). 🇬🇧
