## Big Shoulders has been added to Google Fonts.

*Image: © Big Shoulders*

The [display](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Big+Shoulders+Display?fbclid=IwAR1utEGl_hbtPv9q2-FMC-Vl18-XCZFE14TZoWqQq05Sw0EEJh5PTHWHEuY) and [text](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Big+Shoulders+Text) versions of the Big Shoulders typeface, designed by [Patric King](https://twitter.com/pksucks), have been added to Google Fonts. Stated to have been created for the city of Chicago and its citizens, the Open Font License (OFL) Big Shoulders is offered to users in eight weights for each version. Although there are notable flaws in the typeface, particularly with diacritical marks, it is open to development via its [GitHub page](https://github.com/xotypeco/big_shoulders).

## Custom fonts can be installed on Apple devices with iOS 13 and iPad OS.

*Image: © [Apple](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=psL_5RIBqnY)*

Apple is offering users the custom font installation feature, introduced at the [WWDC 2019](https://youtu.be/psL_5RIBqnY?t=4226) event this past June, with iOS 13 and iPad OS. Known for its closed ecosystem, Apple previously had no official way to install fonts on mobile operating systems. As a result of Apple’s collaboration with companies like [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/tr/), [Monotype](https://www.monotype.com), and [DynaFont](https://www.dynacw.com/en/netshop/netshop.aspx), system-level font installation is now officially supported on phones and tablet devices. To use this feature, font providers or type foundries need to build an app or embed this functionality into an existing app.

One of the first apps to support the new font installation feature is [Font Diner](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/font-diner/id1480286620). Currently allowing you to install a free bundle of fonts for personal use only, the app offers a good and easy way to try out the font installation feature. Fonts installed with user consent are located under the **Settings → General → Fonts** menu. The alphabetical list of fonts, file size, and copyright information can be seen under this menu. Additionally, you can remove the font from the system without needing the installed app. It should be kept in mind that uninstalling a font installation app will also remove all fonts that came with the app. It is also worth noting that custom fonts being installed with this new method will not automatically be available in every app that uses system fonts. For this, apps that allow the use of custom fonts need to be selected. In the future, most apps may implement this, but you may need to wait for future app updates for custom fonts to appear as an option in the font menu of specific applications. Users who wish to try this feature can check out Apple’s built-in app *Pages*, which already supports custom font installation. (Source: [Typography.Guru](http://Typography.Guru))

> You can watch the relevant segment of the WWDC 2019 event where the font installation feature was introduced [here](https://youtu.be/psL_5RIBqnY?t=4226).

## A platform for crowdfunding open font licensed typefaces: Public Type Works

*Image: © [Public Type Works](https://www.publictype.works)*

[Public Type Works](https://www.publictype.works), a platform aiming to produce open font licensed (OFL) typefaces through crowdfunding, has launched. The platform works as follows: The type designer determines how many backers they want to receive support from and uploads images showing what the typeface they will design will look like. After receiving the anticipated support, the completed typeface is published under the Open Font License (OFL) and sent to the backers.

## New book from Mark van Wageningen: “Type and Color: How to Design and Use Multicolored Typefaces”

*Image: © [Type and Color: How to Design and Use Multicolored Typefaces.](https://amzn.to/2oMsns8)*

Type designers generally think in black and white. Written by Dutch graphic designer [Mark van Wageningen](https://twitter.com/NovoTypo), [“Type and Color: How to Design and Use Multicolored Typefaces”](https://amzn.to/2oMsns8) upends this notion. In the book published in 2019 by *Princeton Architectural Press*, Wageningen offers all the tools needed to design and develop your own multicolored typefaces. Containing guidebook-level insights on color type—which we will hear much more about in the future alongside variable fonts—the book also covers color theories and the fundamentals of type design.

## New book from Slanted: “The Yearbook of Type 2019/20”

*Image: © [The Yearbook of Type 2019/20](https://www.yearbookoftype.com)*

Providing a practical guide to staying informed about current type design trends and innovations, and to choosing the right typeface for a project, [The Yearbook of Type 2019/20](https://www.yearbookoftype.com) was published by [Slanted Publishers](https://www.slanted.de). Through examples, background information, technical details, instructions, and descriptions from 176 type designers across 36 countries, along with a series of essays showcasing the latest trends in contemporary type design, the world of contemporary typography is presented to the reader.

## New typeface for Nature magazine: Harding

*Image: © [Logo of Nature Magazine in 1869 and 2019](https://go.nature.com/2PdtHPe)*

In honor of its 150th anniversary, the world-renowned scientific journal [Nature](https://www.nature.com) [refreshed its design](https://go.nature.com/2PdtHPe) under the direction of creative director [Kelly Krause](https://twitter.com/kellybkrause). For Nature’s new design, a custom typeface named *Harding* was also designed by [Commercial Type](https://commercialtype.com). Featuring special symbols used in scientific papers, Harding was designed screen-first with an x-height aimed at providing maximum legibility and short ascenders and descenders that help fit long strings of words into small spaces.

## London Underground commemorates the famous typeface Johnston.

*Image: © [Fraser Muggeridge](https://www.instagram.com/frasermuggeridgestudio/)*

To commemorate the contribution made to urban memory by [Edward Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Johnston), creator of the London Underground’s famous typeface [Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_(typeface)), giant [wooden letters were hung](http://bit.ly/2NcNpIB) at Farringdon Station. In the work designed by [Fraser Muggeridge](https://www.instagram.com/frasermuggeridgestudio/), when looking closely at the letters hung in reverse as they were used in printing, Johnston’s name can be seen.
