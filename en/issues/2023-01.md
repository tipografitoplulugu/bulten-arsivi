## A book on women’s contributions to print culture: “Women in Print 2”

*Image: © [Women in Print 2](https://www.kobo.com/tr/tr/ebook/women-in-print-2)*

The article titled *Ottoman Women’s Print Network and Their Creative Contribution to Print Culture in Turkey*, co-authored by one of our founders, [Ömer Durmaz](https://twitter.com/omerdurmaz), and [Özlem Özkal](https://ozlemozkal.me/), has been published in the book “[Women in Print 2](https://www.kobo.com/tr/tr/ebook/women-in-print-2)”. Featured in the book published by [Peter Lang Publishers](https://www.peterlang.com/) and focusing on the creative contributions of women to print culture in the Ottoman Empire, the article was also presented as a paper at the *Women in Print* conference held at the University of Birmingham in 2018. 🇬🇧

## Anatomy of Arabic letters

*Image: © [TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com/arabic-type-anatomy)*

Azza Alameddine’s [article](https://www.type-together.com/arabic-type-anatomy) on the anatomy of Arabic letters. 🇬🇧 Stating that there is no common terminology for Arabic letters compared to Latin typography, Alameddine takes a step toward standardizing the terminology of Arabic typography.

## Can an alphabet save a culture?

*Image: © [Microsoft](https://unlocked.microsoft.com/adlam-can-an-alphabet-save-a-culture/)*

For an ancient people, a new alphabet promises to preserve their culture and connect their communities. Creating a new alphabet for the Fulani people of West Africa, known as *Pulaar*, the brothers Ibrahima and Abdoulaye Barry [designed](https://unlocked.microsoft.com/adlam-can-an-alphabet-save-a-culture/) an alphabet they named ADLaM, meaning *the alphabet that protects people from disappearance*. 🇬🇧

## How to use clashing fonts?

*Image: © Hoefler&Co*

Designers are trained to believe that similar fonts should never be used together. However, breaking this fundamental rule can sometimes be a good way to create orderly, elegant typography. In his latest [article](https://www.typography.com/blog/fonts-that-clash), Jonathan Hoefler discusses how to use contrasting typefaces together. 🇬🇧

## How can typography create a more inclusive future?

*Image: © [*It’s Nice That*](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/forward-thinking-how-typography-can-make-a-more-inclusive-future-graphic-design-040123)*

In his [article](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/forward-thinking-how-typography-can-make-a-more-inclusive-future-graphic-design-040123), designer [Ray Masaki](http://r-m.work/) notes that despite progress being made, the type industry still has a long way to go, arguing that the future of type design will depend less on tools and technology and more on the decisions made by type designers. 🇬🇧

## How does typography affect your mood?

*Image: © [Sarah Hyndman](https://shyndman.medium.com/how-typography-impacts-your-mood-5bbc3386de03)*

In her [article](https://shyndman.medium.com/how-typography-impacts-your-mood-5bbc3386de03) on how typography affects people’s moods, Sarah Hyndman, founder of [TypeTasting](https://www.typetasting.com/), points out that just as we physically use our voices and facial expressions to convey emotional cues ranging from subtle to dramatic, typefaces and the ways they are used provide a similarly wide emotional range typographically. 🇬🇧

## Is it possible to rethink a German design icon?

*Image: © [Fontwerk](https://neuedin.com/)*

Hendrik Weber, Andreas Frohloff, and Olli Meier redesigned the German design icon [DIN](https://neuedin.com/) typeface for the Berlin-based type foundry [Fontwerk](https://fontwerk.com/en/). With particular emphasis on its Berlin design heritage, the typeface combines the requirements of the digital world with a variable-font-first design approach. Additionally, 10% of the profits from the typeface’s sales will be donated to non-profit climate protection organizations advocating for political and technological change, notably *Future Cleantech Architects* and *Carbon180*.

## US Department of State switches from Times New Roman to Calibri.

*Image: © [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2023/01/18/state-department-times-new-roman-calibri/)*

According to a [report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2023/01/18/state-department-times-new-roman-calibri/) by The Washington Post, the US Department of State instructed its staff domestically and abroad to phase out the Times New Roman typeface and use Calibri in official communications and memos to assist employees with visual impairments or reading difficulties. In their [article](https://readabilitymatters.org/articles/the-times-new-roman-are-a-changing) on the subject, *Readability Matters* suggests that giving readers the option to adjust typeface characteristics to suit their own needs is a better solution. 🇬🇧

## 2022 report from Dinamo

*Image: © [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com/news/the-dinamo-annual-report)*

Dinamo Type Foundry released its [2022 report](https://abcdinamo.com/news/the-dinamo-annual-report). The report reveals that its top-selling typeface, *Favorit*, has been dethroned, no single typeface accounted for more than 15% of sales, and sales were evenly distributed across the typefaces in the foundry’s catalog. 🇬🇧
