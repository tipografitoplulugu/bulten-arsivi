## TipoGráfica’s twenty-year archive is now online

The twenty-year archive of Argentina’s typography magazine *TipoGráfica*, published between 1987 and 2007, has been [made available](http://bit.ly/2jVzKuG) online to researchers. Published with the support of [Fontana Design Studio](http://fontana-d.com), the archive will soon be accompanied by images and texts from events such as conferences and biennials, supported by English translations.

## 2019 SOTA Typography Award presented to Paul Shaw.

Designer and historian [Paul Shaw](http://www.paulshawletterdesign.com) was named [this year’s winner of the SOTA Typography Award](https://www.typesociety.org/typography/2019/), presented by [The Society of Typographic Aficionados](https://www.typesociety.org) since 2003. Shaw, who has taught at various universities and schools in the New York area since 1980—including the School of Visual Arts, Parsons, and [Type@Cooper](http://coopertype.org)—has taken part in the design of eighteen typefaces, including *Kolo, Donatello, Bermuda, Old Claude*, and *Stockholm*.

## Geometric font crafted with code: Leon Sans

Created with code by [Jongmin Kim](https://cmiscm.com) in 2019, the geometric sans-serif font [Leon Sans](https://leon-kim.com/) allows you to dynamically change weight and create custom animations, effects, or shapes in the HTML5 canvas element. Detailed information can be found on the [GitHub page](https://github.com/cmiscm/leonsans) of Leon Sans, where every point has coordinate values.

## Variable typeface for coding and interface design: Recursive

Starting in 2018 as [Stephen Nixon](https://twitter.com/ThunderNixon)’s master’s thesis project in the [TypeMedia](http://typemedia.org) graduate program, [Recursive](https://www.recursive.design) is a variable typeface designed for coding and interface design. Representing one of Google Fonts’ steps toward variable typefaces, Recursive can be followed on its [GitHub page](https://github.com/arrowtype/recursive).

## Typotheque turns 20!

Founded by Johanna Biľak, Peter Biľak, and Nikola Djurek, type foundry [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com) is celebrating its twentieth anniversary this September with new announcements. [The first of these](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/typotheque_20th_edge_20th) is the re-release of a revised version of *Omar Sans*—which they designed in 1999 and retired in 2001—under the name *Edge Sans*… Designed by Ermin Međedović across nine weights, Edge Sans has an anatomy that pushes the concept of geometry to extremes on an orthogonal grid.

## Lautsprecher gets its voice back.

In an article published on [Letterform Archive](http://letterformarchive.org), Stephen Coles [noted](http://bit.ly/2lV2ynF) the transformation of [Jakob Erbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Erbar)’s little-known typeface *Lautsprecher* (*Ger.* Loudspeaker)—released just before World War II—from archival documents into a digital typeface. Released in a single weight in 1931, Lautsprecher was reissued by [David Jonathan Ross](https://twitter.com/djrrb) in September 2019 exclusively for the [Font of Month Club](http://fontofthemonth.club) under the name [Lautsprecher DJR](https://djr.com/lautsprecher/lautsprecher-djr-specimen.pdf).

## Sakarya’s brush sign painter: Enver Yücebalkan

[Murat Ertürk](http://www.muraterturk.com)’s article, which uncovers the life and works of brush sign painter *Enver Yücebalkan*—who was active in Sakarya between 1946 and 1998—starting from a single signboard, was [published.](http://bit.ly/2Wlujq9) in the Proceedings Book of the [1st International Ahmet Yakupoğlu Symposium on City, Art and Design](http://ahmetyakupoglu.org/anasayfa/) Documenting the place of the sign painter, who also painted movie posters, in the city’s visual culture and design history, Ertürk aims to raise awareness of vernacular typography through graphic artifacts produced with disappearing traditional methods.

## Adobe ends Type 1 support.

Adobe [announced](https://adobe.ly/2kbCCDY) that it will end support for Type 1 fonts in 2021. As a result, fonts in [Type 1](https://www.prepressure.com/fonts/basics/type1) format will no longer be listed in Photoshop’s font menu. Introduced in 1984 alongside [PostScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostScript) and [Apple LaserWriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaserWriter), the Type 1 font format was superseded in 2000 by OpenType ([opentype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenType)) fonts, which retained all the advantages of Type 1 while offering cross-platform compatibility and advanced typographic features.

## Wim Crouwel has passed away.

Iconic graphic designer and typographer [Wim Crouwel](http://www.iconofgraphics.com/Wim-Crouwel/) has [passed away](http://bit.ly/2mmK67G) in his native Amsterdam at the age of 90.

Born in the Netherlands in 1928, Crouwel studied at Academie Minerva in Groningen, Netherlands, between 1947 and 1949. He began his career in 1955 designing exhibitions, graphics, and products together with Kho Liang Ie. In 1963, he became one of the founders of the design studio [Total Design](https://www.totaldesign.com). In 1967, he designed the typeface New Alphabet. In 1970, he designed the Netherlands pavilion for Expo '70 in Osaka. In addition to graphic design, he was actively involved in education. Crouwel’s work is especially recognized for its grid-based layout and use of typography in the [International Typographic Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Typographic_Style).

## Alexander Artsvuni: “UI vs. Print: Contradictions in Typographical Practice”

“For a 10-point font, normal line spacing should be 12 points.” Are the rules recommended for print publications and inherited from movable type culture valid in the digital world? ## New typeface from renowned type foundry Hoefler&Co: Decimal

Renowned type foundry [Hoefler&Co](https://www.typography.com), famous for its library of nearly fifteen hundred typefaces, has announced its new typeface [Decimal](http://bit.ly/2ltXgzD). Inspired by the lettering styles used on the dials of vintage watches, Decimal features an extensive family. In addition, type designer [Jonathan Hoefler](https://twitter.com/jonathanhoefler) and the Hoefler&Co team are featured with Decimal’s design process in the sixth episode of the second season of the Netflix documentary [Abstract: The Art of Design](https://youtu.be/PHXxKZkeFmc), which was nominated for an Emmy in *Outstanding Graphic Design and Art Direction*.

## Riccardo Olocco: “Non-Jensonian Early Venetian Romans”

Riccardo Olocco has published the paper he presented at the symposium organized in April 2019 by [ANRT](https://gotico-antiqua.anrt-nancy.fr/en) ([the Atelier National de Recherche Typographique](https://gotico-antiqua.anrt-nancy.fr/en)) on his [Medium](http://bit.ly/2mJiqdo) page. In his article, Olocco shares details of his ongoing research on roman types cut in Venice in the fifteenth century, offering a comparison between the type used by [Nicolas Jenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Jenson)—which can be regarded as the prototype of roman type—and other *peculiar* types cut in Venice between 1470 and 1473.
