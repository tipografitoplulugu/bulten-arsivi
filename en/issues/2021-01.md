## A new look from the CIA

*Image: © [CIA](https://twitter.com/CIA/status/1344933081287974912)*

The CIA [announced](https://twitter.com/CIA/status/1344933081287974912) that it transitioned to a new look with the new year. The CIA’s new look features the typefaces [America](https://www.grillitype.com/typeface/gt-america) and [Sectra](https://www.grillitype.com/typeface/gt-sectra) from Grilli Type, a Swiss type foundry founded in 2009 by *Noël Leu* and *Thierry Blancpain*. Grilli Type, however, announced that they had no role in the new look and that their typefaces were licensed by an advertising agency. The CIA did not provide information on who designed its new web page.

## Mongolia abandons its Soviet past by changing its alphabet.

*Image: © [The Times.](https://www.thetimes.co.uk/article/mongolia-abandons-soviet-past-by-restoring-alphabet-rsvcgqmxd)*

Mongolia [announced](https://www.thetimes.co.uk/article/mongolia-abandons-soviet-past-by-restoring-alphabet-rsvcgqmxd) plans to transition to its traditional alphabet by 2025, replacing the Cyrillic alphabet designated by the Soviet Union to escape Russian influence. In the Mongolian People’s Republic, which adopted the Cyrillic alphabet in the 1940s, it was thought that the traditional Mongolian alphabet would replace the Cyrillic alphabet after the 1990 Mongolian revolution, but the plan was ultimately scrapped.

## Type design for SALT by Cem Eskinazi

*Image: © [SALT](http://saltonline.org/tr/2313)*

The fifteenth edition of Kraliçe—the type design program organized by SALT—designed by type designer Cem Eskinazi, [whom we interviewed last November](https://medium.com/tipografi/söyleşi-cem-eskinazi-a8794f1afde4), titled [Sohbet](http://saltonline.org/tr/2313), was published on the tenth anniversary of SALT’s founding. Developed based on *Queen*, designed by Timo Gaessner, Kraliçe consists of three separate sets open to alternating use, referencing the archives through selected letters and figures.

## Italic font design from Victor Gaultney

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://youtu.be/XkOFwuMBIAA)*

Type designer [Victor Gaultney](https://gaultney.org/jvgtype/) has published his presentation on designing italic fonts, prepared for [ATypI](https://www.atypi.org)’s *All Over* event held between October 27–31, 2020, on YouTube. [Gaultney](https://gaultney.org/jvgtype/)’s five-year research into historical and contemporary design practices of italics, conducted as part of his master's thesis at the University of Reading—including interviews with more than twenty contemporary designers—can be accessed via his [personal website](https://gaultney.org/jvgtype/italics/designing-italics/).

## Type and Media 2020 projects published.

*Image: © Detail from Nina Botthof’s typeface [Ceres](https://2020.typemedia.org/nina-botthof/)*

Projects produced in 2020 within [Type and Media](https://www.kabk.nl/en/programmes/master/type-and-media/), the postgraduate type design program of [The Royal Academy of Art](https://www.kabk.nl/en/) (KABK) in The Hague, have begun to be exhibited. The postgraduate program's instructors include leading figures in the field such as Erik van Blokland, Peter Verheul, Paul van der Laan, Peter Bil’ak, and Just van Rossum. You can visit [2020.typemedia.org](https://2020.typemedia.org) to view the typefaces developed by twelve students as part of their graduation projects.

## Johann David Steingruber’s architectural alphabets

*Image: © [*Architectonisches Alphabet*](http://bit.ly/36c65SR)*

In 1773, Johann David Steingruber (1702–1787) created building and site plans composed of alphabetical letterforms. You can access the entire *Architectonisches Alphabet* (Architectural Alphabet) document, digitized by the Austrian National Library, via [this link](http://bit.ly/36c65SR).
