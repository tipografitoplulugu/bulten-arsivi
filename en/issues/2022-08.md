# Typography Newsletter: August 2022

### A monthly summary of notable developments in the world of typography

⏰ **Reading Time:** ~4 minutes

Hello everyone!

Highlights, new typefaces, and developments from the world of typography in the Turkish Typography Society’s August 2022 bulletin:

---

## On tight tracking

Noting that clients have recently favored tight tracking and designers view it as an effective way to make any message appear faster and more energetic, [Hoefler&Co](https://www.typography.com) wrote [an article worth reading](https://www.typography.com/blog/fonts-for-tight-tracking) touching upon several points to consider when adjusting tracking. 🇬🇧

---

## Will and Schumacher’s wood type production

Leipzig-based designer and co-founder of [Forgotten Shapes](https://forgotten-shapes.com), [Pierre Pané-Farré](https://panefarre.com), [offers](https://klim.co.nz/blog/case-will-schumacher/) an excellent insight into typographic research through a rare, undated 19th-century wood type specimen from Will and Schumacher—manufacturers of industrial wood type in Continental Europe—housed in the [Ghent University Library](https://lib.ugent.be/en/catalog/rug01:001762370?i=0&q=Will+%26+Schumacher). 🇬🇧 Pané-Farré has also turned this research into a limited-edition 28-page [publication](https://panefarre.com/2022/08/01/the-case-of-will-schumacher/).

---

## Color letterpress archive

The color letterpress printing [examples](https://www.sheaff-ephemera.com/list/artistic_printing_album/) in the archive of designer and ephemera collector Richard D. Sheaff are well worth examining. 🇬🇧

---

## Abdullah Taşcı Retrospective Exhibition

Selected works created by [Abdullah Taşcı](https://www.abdullahtasci.com.tr/), one of Turkey’s prominent graphic and type designers, over an art and design career spanning more than fifty years, meet visitors in a retrospective exhibition. Featuring nearly a hundred emblems, logos, posters, calligraphy, and type designs bearing Taşcı's signature, the exhibition opened its doors on August 18, 2022. Exhibited in the hall of Mimar Sinan Fine Arts University at Tophane-i Âmire, Taşcı’s works can be visited until September 7, 2022.

---

## Typography, Automation, and the Division of Labor

> “Typography was born with the mass-production mechanism of the printing press; thus, through its perpetual relationship with automation, it has taken part in the distinctly modern dynamics of overwork, underemployment, and runaway production.”

[J. Dakota Brown](https://linktr.ee/jdakota)’s booklet titled “[Typography, Automation, and the Division of Labor](https://otherforms.net/typography-automation-and-the-division-of-labor/)”, published in a limited run in 2019 by [Other Forms Books](https://otherforms.net), in which he reinterprets the history of graphic design by linking it to the history of capitalism, is now available online. Brown, who studied graphic design at North Carolina State University and critical theory at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago, had published a booklet titled “[The Power of Design as a Dream of Autonomy](https://i-n-g-a.com/products/the-power-of-design-as-a-dream-of-autonomy)” in the same year; Ksenya Samarskaya from the *AIGA Eye on Design* team also conducted an [interview](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/designer-educator-j-dakota-brown-is-untangling-the-relationship-between-design-and-labor/) with Brown in 2021. 🇬🇧

## New typography book: “The Typographic Medium”

New York University faculty member [Kate Brideau](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/faculty/bio/kate-brideau), in her book titled “[The Typographic Medium](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262045858/)” published by [The MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/) in 2021, presents an innovative examination of typography as a medium of communication rather than merely a component of print or digital media. Examining the tension between the often-overlooked *typographic form* and the frequently dominant *function*, Brideau argues that typography consists of shapes rather than letters, and that these shapes are existentially and technologically at the heart of the typographic medium. 🇬🇧

## More Latin type design for African languages

As part of its goal to improve the experience of Sub-Saharan African users and to provide more choices among the few open-source fonts available, Google has released the typeface [Questrial](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Questrial) for all African languages that use the Latin writing system. Originally designed by [Joe Prince](https://dribbble.com/JoePrince) and expanded by [Laura Meseguer](https://www.laurameseguer.com), Susanna Zaraysky has written an [article](https://design.google/library/african-latin/) detailing Questrial’s design process. 🇬🇧

## On Italy’s typographic modernism during the fascist era

Graphic designer, educator, and design critic [Silvia Sfligiotti](https://www.alizarina.net/info/silvia-sfligiotti-cv/) has written [an article worth reading](https://fontstand.com/news/essays/a-matter-of-taste/) on the typographic modernism of graphic designers living in 1930s fascist Italy, who developed a distinctly Italian modernist visual language across fascist propaganda exhibitions and industrial advertising. 🇬🇧

## XYZ Type refreshed the lettering logo of Rolling Stone magazine.

XYZ Type has [refreshed](https://xyztype.com/custom/project/rolling_stone) the lettering in the logo of Rolling Stone magazine, a pioneer of cultural and political news from the perspective of music and musicians since it began publication by Jann Wenner in 1967. 🇬🇧 The logo was commissioned in 2021 by Roger Black, who was Rolling Stone’s art director between 1976 and 1978 and is currently the president of Type Network.

---

## Monotype acquires Berthold’s renowned typefaces.

Akzidenz-Grotesk, one of the world’s first widely used sans-serif typefaces that inspired many subsequent type designs including Helvetica, [joins](https://www.monotype.com/company/news/monotype-acquires-bertholds-renowned-typeface-inventory) the Monotype family along with more than 700 historic typefaces from the Berthold library. 🇬🇧 The inventory and digital typeface distribution rights of the [Berthold Type Foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berthold_Type_Foundry), founded in Berlin by Hermann Berthold in 1858, were acquired in 1997 by [Berthold Types Limited](https://www.bertholdtypes.com). Alongside Akzidenz-Grotesk, this inventory also includes beloved typefaces such as Imago, Cosmos, Formata, Block, Solemnis, and City.
