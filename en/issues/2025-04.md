## New typeface from Yaprak Buse Çağlar: Aksan

[Yaprak Buse Çağlar](https://www.instagram.com/typolea/), one of the 2023–2025 graduates of [ÉSAD Type](https://esadtype.esad-amiens.fr/)—the postgraduate program in type design run by ÉSAD Amiens (École supérieure d’art et de design d’Amiens)—designed a versatile type family named [Aksan](https://esadtype.esad-amiens.fr/aksan/) as her graduation project, reflecting the transformation of writing and the evolution of typography. Designed to cover many languages using the Latin alphabet, Aksan combines strong visual references of the past with today’s design sensibilities for a typeface that is both nostalgic and modern, inspired by the Alphabet Reform in Turkey. In addition, Yaprak was also a [speaker](https://atypi.org/presentation/a-study-on-the-change-of-turkish-alphabet-research-on-a-cultural-identity/) at the conference organized by [ATypI](https://atypi.org/conferences-events/atypi-copenhagen-2025/) in Copenhagen between April 22–26, 2025.

## Comic Sans and the art of imperfection

> “Comic Sans wasn’t meant to start a revolution.”

Even though Comic Sans has a bad reputation—which it never deserved—it actually served its intended design purpose quite well. In 1994, American type designer Vinnie Connare noticed Times New Roman being used in a speech balloon for Rover the dog in the Microsoft Bob software, thought that dogs don’t talk in Times New Roman, and designed the casual, friendly, and handwriting-like Comic Sans. [In her article, Danielle McClune](https://microsoft.design/articles/comic-sans-and-the-art-of-imperfection/) states that the imperfections of Comic Sans actually highlight its human and accessible qualities, which is why it holds a unique place in the design world.

## The King of France’s exclusive typeface: “Le Romain du Roi”

> “The exclusive typeface of the Royal Printing House, Romain du Roi, was constructed with a set square and compass as the result of decades of work, and initiated the Transitional (Réales) Period that developed during the Age of Enlightenment.”

In an article published on [Typofonderie](https://typofonderie.com/), French graphic and typography historian Michel Wlassikoff explores the history and design process of the “[Le Romain du Roi](https://typofonderie.com/gazette/le-romain-du-roi-the-exclusive-typeface-of-louis-xiv)” typeface, commissioned in 1692 by King Louis XIV of France for use by the Royal Printing House.

## A $30,000 bill from Monotype

Monotype [raised](https://www.reddit.com/r/graphic_design/comments/1jqqlm6/monotype_just_hit_us_with_a_30000yr_font_license/?rdt=44913) the annual license fee for Avenir, which a non-profit organization had been using for ten years, from $4,000 to $30,000. As justification, it cited font access by five employees and freelance designers, along with 150,000 monthly pageviews on the website, as a *license violation*. Furthermore, it announced that if they do not accept the new fee, it will demand retroactive usage fees for the past ten years. The debates, sparked by [Grilli Type’s post on X](https://x.com/grillitype/status/1908232294029992270), brought the complexity of font licensing and Monotype’s aggressive policies back to the forefront. Debates over Monotype’s monopolization had been on the agenda before, and we had published an [article](https://medium.com/tipografi/yaz%C4%B1-end%C3%BCstrisinde-tekelle%C5%9Fme-f100fe18760b) on this topic.

## “Further Reading” Symposium

On April 25, 2025, the Department of Design at Germany's [University of Applied Sciences Potsdam](https://www.fh-potsdam.de/en) hosted the fourth edition of the symposium titled “[Further Reading](https://furtherreading.de/)”. ## A typeface for kids: Kermit

Microsoft [introduced](https://microsoft.design/articles/introducing-kermit-a-typeface-for-kids/) a new typeface called *Kermit* to improve children’s reading skills. Developed by [Underware Type Foundry](https://www.underware.nl/), Kermit specifically aims to help children with reading difficulties, such as dyslexia.

## Better typesetting on the web with “Text Wrap”

The WebKit team [introduced](https://webkit.org/blog/16547/better-typography-with-text-wrap-pretty/) a new CSS property called `text-wrap: pretty `to improve web typography. This feature ensures that text blocks look more balanced and aesthetic by preventing widows on the last line of paragraphs. In addition, it reduces rag at line ends and minimizes unnecessary hyphenation.

## Emigre’s type specimens

Emigre has published the type specimen “[Whatever](https://www.emigre.com/TypeSpecimens/Whatever)”, which features visuals from thirty-seven type families released in the 1990s and uses slang expressions and names of pop culture phenomena that defined the era. You can download the free PDF version from the Emigre website or purchase a printed copy. Don’t forget to check out [other specimens](https://www.emigre.com/TypeSpecimens) as well.

## New series from Letterform Archive: “Type By”

Letterform Archive has prepared a special three-volume collection titled “[Type By](https://letterformarchive.org/news/inside-the-type-by-series-bernhard-excoffon-and-novarese/)”, honoring the work of leading twentieth-century type designers Lucian Bernhard (1883–1972), Roger Excoffon (1910–1983), and Aldo Novarese (1920–1995). This collection offers an in-depth look into the history of typography by re-presenting rare type specimens from the movable type era in their original sizes and designs.

## The colorful journey of typography from Gaby Bazin

Written and illustrated by illustrator Gaby Bazin, “[Meet the Typographer](https://www.davidzwirner.com/collect/meet-the-typographer-book)” reveals the fascinating evolution and technique of movable type printing with colorful and engaging illustrations. Serving as a sequel to “[Meet the Lithographer](https://www.davidzwirner.com/collect/meet-the-lithographer-book)”, the book is published by *David Zwirner Books*.

## A new book from Türkiye İş Bankası Kültür Yayınları

Following Steven Roger Fischer’s book “[A History of Writing](https://www.iskultur.com.tr/yazinin-tarihi.aspx)”, “[A History of Reading](https://www.iskultur.com.tr/okumanin-tarihi.aspx)” has also been published by Türkiye İş Bankası Kültür Yayınları. Translated by A. Handan Konar, the book examines the different forms and meanings of reading, from the complexity of Mesopotamian cuneiform to reading aloud in Ancient Egypt, from the codex culture that spread across the Roman Empire to the printed book revolution of the Renaissance, and finally to the screens of the internet era.

---

## Type creation tool: Grid Type

Inspired by the grid-based stencil *Plaque Découpée Universelle* developed by Joseph A. David in 1876, the experimental type design tool [Grid Type](https://www.grid-type.com/) lets you create different types of grids, draw your letters within these grids, and download them as a font file.

Applications have opened for the [Gerard Unger Scholarship](https://www.type-together.com/gerard-unger-scholarship) awarded by TypeTogether. Open until June 1, any student who is enrolled in or graduating from a typography course this year and has fully or partially developed a type family of multiple fonts that includes, but is not limited to, the Latin alphabet may apply for the scholarship.
