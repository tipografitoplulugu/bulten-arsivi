## Recognizing Japanese characters with artificial intelligence

Dr. Tarin Clanuwat, a Thai scholar and specialist in medieval Japanese literature, has [developed](https://news.fontstand.com/essays/kuzushiji-recognition-with-ai/) an AI-based character recognition system that magically translates pre-modern Japanese characters into modern Japanese. 🇬🇧

## Paula Scher designs Helvetica NFTs with Monotype.

Monotype invited artists and designers to contribute to a collection celebrating the omnipresence of one of the world’s best-known typefaces and the role of typography in culture. The series was produced in partnership with the Web 3.0 creative community [KnownUnknown](https://knownunknown.com) and will be released as NFTs in the coming weeks. One of the invitees, renowned graphic designer and partner at [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com), Paula Scher, [designed](https://www.pentagram.com/news/paula-scher-creates-helvetica-nfts-in-collaboration-with-monotype) a series of NFTs as part of Helvetica Now, Monotype’s first digital art collection. Scher’s three NFTs, titled “Blowing Up Helvetica,” were inspired by the [Helvetica family](https://catalog.monotype.com/family/linotype/helvetica), spanning many weights from the thinnest to the boldest. 🇬🇧

## A variable font version of Franklin Gothic has been designed.

Founded by [Nick Sherman](https://nicksherman.com), the [HEX Project](https://hex.xyz) has [announced](https://www.futurefonts.xyz/hex/hex-franklin) a variable font version paying homage to the [Franklin Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Gothic) type family and its siblings, News Gothic and Lightline Gothic. A [dedicated page](https://hex.xyz/HEX_Franklin/Tyght/) was also designed to test the variable version of Franklin Gothic. 🇬🇧

## A faster reading experience with Bionic Reading

Developed by Swiss designer [Renato Casutt](https://twitter.com/renato_casutt) as a result of six years of research, [Bionic Reading](https://bionic-reading.com/) claims to provide faster reading by guiding the brain to complete the rest of the word when its initial letters are highlighted. The project, which began development in 2016, was supported by the Swiss InnoSuisse Institute, and it was announced that a small preliminary study would be conducted with a Swiss university in the coming months. However, criticism of this claim was not long in coming. Prof. Lauren M. Singer Trakhman from the University of Maryland [wrote](https://theconversation.com/can-bionic-reading-make-you-a-speed-reader-not-so-fast-183905) that the sample size of 12 was too small, that it had not been tested on people with dyslexia, and that the claim was overstated since it had not been subjected to standard scientific testing. Ralf Hermann published a critical [video](https://youtu.be/V53tbzy9n9w) on his YouTube channel. Meanwhile, the Readwise team conducted a quick experiment to test whether Bionic Reading actually works, and early results [show](https://blog.readwise.io/does-bionic-reading-actually-work/) that the speed-reading claim is not entirely accurate. 🇬🇧

## p98a may be forced to close!

[Erik Spiekermann](https://twitter.com/espiekermann) announced that he would be forced to close the [p98a](https://www.p98a.com) letterpress workshop, which he founded in Berlin years ago, if he cannot secure financial support by the end of the year, and that he is asking for help from his network in finding support.

## The story of the smallest metal type used by Ottoman printing houses

In his [new article](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/iusty/issue/71138/1066671) published in the journal *Sanat Tarihi Yıllığı*, Onur Yazıcıgil examines the 8-point Naskh typeface—cut and cast in Istanbul between 1870 and 1875 and used in various typographic layouts across Ottoman printing houses—and its typefounder Mehmet Emin Efendi.

## A film on sign painting

Created by Faythe Levine and Sam Macon, the [film](http://www.signpaintersfilm.com/#watch), which takes an intimate look at the past, present, and future of the sign painting industry in the US, is enriched by the narratives of artists across the country, including Ira Coyne, Bob Dewhurst, Keith Knecht, Norma Jeane Maloney, and Stephen Powers.🇬🇧

## A tool allowing you to compare the best Swiss typefaces: Typologie

Created by Paris-based design studio [Recto Verso](https://rectoverso.studio/), [Typologie](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/recto-verso-typologie-swiss-foundries-edition-graphic-design-130622) allows you to easily compare the styles and characteristics of different type foundries. Both a catalog and a creative tool enabling readers to test type combinations for headlines and body text, Typologie features a total of 80 typefaces from 18 type foundries, including Switzerland’s [Grilli Type](https://www.grillitype.com) and [Dinamo](https://abcdinamo.com). 🇬🇧

## Emma Marichal wins the Gerard Unger Scholarship.

French type designer [Emma Marichal](https://emmamarichal.fr) [won](https://www.type-together.com/2022-gerard-unger-scholarship-results) the eighth edition of the Gerard Unger Scholarship with her typeface *Ploquine*. Ploquine was designed during Marichal’s postgraduate studies in type design at Ésad d’Amiens under the mentorship of Sébastien Morlighem. Intended for use in editorial design and inspired by wood type from the specimen books of French type foundries, the type family consists of one variable font and five weights.🇬🇧

## New section from Font Knowledge: The history of type

“[The History of Type](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/history_of_type),” the next installment of Google’s [Font Knowledge](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge) project, has been published. Prepared by [Micah Rich](https://twitter.com/micahbrich) and [Thomas Jockin](https://twitter.com/ThomasJockin) and consisting of three articles, the module takes an in-depth look at the history of type, providing an understanding of how type works. 🇬🇧

## Results of the TDC Ascenders announced.

The results of [Ascenders](https://www.tdc.org/news/tdc-announces-25-winners-for-global-ascenders-3-competition/), organized by the Type Directors Club, have been announced. Open to international typographers, calligraphers, and type designers aged 35 and under, the competition announced 25 talented winners from 13 countries. On August 16, 2022, The One Club in New York will host a reception for the winners, and the exhibition opened as part of the competition will run until September 1, 2022.

## A font created from Hong Kong’s road signs: Prison Gothic

In a project initiated by Gary Yau with the *Road Research Society* that captures the spirit of a city while also raising broader social questions, the characters from road signs handmade by prisoners in Hong Kong prior to computerized production processes have been transformed into a font called [Prison Gothic](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/prison-gothic-hong-kong-road-signs-reborn-as-new-font?mc_cid=0410be2cfe&mc_eid=48a95a3cf1). 🇬🇧
