## A revival initiative: The Rosart Project

Developed by Walda Verbaenen, Michel Paré, and Lukas Schneider, [The Rosart Project](https://www.rosart.nl) focuses on reviving the work of 18th-century Belgian typographer [Jacques-François Rosart](http://luc.devroye.org/fonts-39092.html) (1714–1777). Initiated during the 2014–2015 academic year at the [Plantin Institute of Typography](http://www.plantininstitute.be) in Belgium by five students taking the type design course led by Dr. Frank E. Blokland, the project stands out as the largest and most comprehensive revival initiative based on the work of a single typographer.

## Jan van Toorn passed away.

One of the pioneers of Dutch graphic design, Jan van Toorn (1932–2020), passed away at the age of 88. Considered alongside [Wim Crouwel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wim_Crouwel) as one of the pioneers of Dutch modern graphic design, van Toorn studied at the Amsterdam Graphic School and the Gerrit Rietveld Academy, and began his career as a freelance designer in 1957. The debate he held with Wim Crouwel in 1972 on the ethical, political, and cultural implications of graphic design was published as a book titled [The Debate](https://www.amazon.com/Debate-Legendary-Contest-Giants-Graphic/dp/1580934129/ref=sr_1_1?dchild=1&keywords=the+debate+jan+van+toorn&qid=1605614836&sr=8-1).

## A new book from Kris Sowersby: “The Art of Letters”

> “There is no definitive form of the alphabet. The alphabet is a concept, made concrete through the countless letterforms that have been written and designed. The alphabet is not expressed by a single typeface, but by all of them. There is a largely unwritten set of rules about how a typeface is put together, on the relationship between letterform and style. There is a balance between a strict system and flexibility.”

Kris Sowersby, founder of [Klim Type Foundry](https://klim.co.nz) and one of the world’s leading type designers, has started crowdfunding for his new book [The Art of Letters](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/formist/kris-sowersby-the-art-of-letters), in which he explores the connection between function and style through letter drawing.

## Insights into the Genzsch & Heyse Type Foundry

Photographs from the 1908 commemorative book of Genzsch & Heyse—founded in Hamburg in 1833 by Johann August Genzsch and operating as an independent type foundry for nearly a century—have been [published](https://www.typografie.info/3/artikel.htm/wissen/einblicke-in-die-schriftgießerei-genzsch-heyse-r459/). With the majority of its shares acquired in 1930 by Bauersche Gießerei, H. Berthold AG, and D. Stempel AG, the Genzsch & Heyse Type Foundry continued to exist until 1963.

## Cemal Akyıldız passed away at the age of 88.

The last master of the manual production era, and the final bridge between publishing illustrators and graphic designers, Cemal Akyıldız passed away at the age of 88 due to the pandemic.

Born in Trabzon in 1932, Cemal Akyıldız moved to Istanbul in 1949 to study at the faculty of fine arts after graduating from commercial high school. He studied under painters such as İbrahim Çallı and Hikmet Onat. Also apprenticing in painters’ studios during that period, Akyıldız interrupted his academic education to establish his own studio in Babıali at the age of 18. Continuing his work by producing book covers, movie posters, illustrations for school textbooks, and calligraphy, Akyıldız designed approximately two thousand novel covers. Having drawn portraits of all Turkish presidents starting with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, Cemal Akyıldız worked as the official artist of Istanbul University for 25 years.

[Cemal Akyıldız](https://youtu.be/P5UYfMhUYps) was featured with his works in the *Cover Illustrators of Babıali* exhibition organized by Ömer Durmaz at Galeri Işık between May 23 and June 21, 2013, and gave an [interview](https://youtu.be/AadekPZCafU) in the sixth installment of the talks organized by GMK on December 21, 2019.

## 39th Graphic Design Exhibition Awards presented.

Organized by GMK and held for the thirty-ninth time this year, the Graphic Design Exhibition Awards found their recipients in an [online ceremony](https://youtu.be/NibfN04meBA) on November 29, 2020. The *Best Typography Award* and the *Sait Maden Typography Award* were presented to Fatih Hardal; the *Typography Achievement Award* to Alp Eren Tekin, Feyza Filiz, and Ruslan Abbas; the *Typeface Design — Display Achievement Award* to Fatih Hardal and Ruslan Abbas; and the *Typeface Design — Text Achievement Award* to Fatih Hardal, Oğuzhan Cengiz, and Taner Ardalı. In this year's open-access online exhibition, 283 designers and 746 designs took part across 22 categories, including Student Projects.

For the 39th Graphic Design Exhibition and this year's award-winning works: [sergi.gmk.org.tr](https://sergi.gmk.org.tr/39)
