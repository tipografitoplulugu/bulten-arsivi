## The winner of the 2021 Gerard Unger Scholarship has been announced.

*Image: © [TypeTogether](https://www.type-together.com/2021-gerard-unger-scholarship-results)*

[Nina Faulhaber](https://www.type-together.com/nina-faulhaber) won the Gerard Unger Scholarship with her typeface [Aeroplan](https://www.type-together.com/2021-gerard-unger-scholarship-results). Designed while Faulhaber was studying communication design at the Augsburg University of Applied Sciences in Germany and formerly named *Flieger*, the typeface was created by reinterpreting a typeface found in a 1916 book on airplane engines.

## Why shouldn’t state school students learn Latin?

*Image: © [Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/voices/latin-state-schools-gavin-williamson-b1895148.html)*

## Custom type family for Starbucks

*Image: © [Lettermatic.com](https://lettermatic.com/custom/starbucks)*

Led by Riley Cran, [Lettermatic](https://lettermatic.com/custom/starbucks) has designed a new typeface for Starbucks. Coordinated by Starbucks creative director Rob Stanton, the 1970s-inspired type family named SoDo was designed. Referencing Starbucks’ own size definitions, it encompasses three weights: *Grande* for large scales, *Tall* for headlines, and *Short* for text.

## Twitter’s new typeface: Chirp

*Image: © [Twitter Design](https://twitter.com/TwitterDesign/status/1425505308563099650)*

Twitter [announced](https://twitter.com/TwitterDesign/status/1425505308563099650) that they have started using their new typeface, Chirp. Begun in January with the goal of designing a flexible, expressive, bold, agile, and fun typeface that reflects the voices making up Twitter, Chirp was developed in collaboration with the renowned type foundry [Grilli Type](https://www.grillitype.com). Noting that versions in Japanese, Chinese, and Arabic are also being developed in addition to Latin, Twitter’s Creative Director [Derrit DeRouen](https://www.derritderouen.com) [recounted](https://twitter.com/DerritDeRouen/status/1354548649561186306) the development process of the new typeface on his personal account. 🇬🇧

The change made by Twitter does not seem to have pleased the majority of users. So much so that 50% of users complained about the change, saying that the new typeface caused headaches and was less legible. Some users were even bewildered by a change that could lead to confusing the *Follow* and *Unfollow* buttons. Following Aaron Mak’s [interview](https://slate.com/technology/2021/08/twitter-font-redesign-chirp-franklin-gothic.html) with 8chan founder Fredrick Brennan to better understand what was going on, Daniel Piper also [addressed](https://www.creativebloq.com/amp/news/new-twitter-font?mc_cid=c09e6e0c9e&mc_eid=48a95a3cf1) user dissatisfaction. 🇬🇧

## Why do we treat and describe letters like humans?

*Image: © [dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/08/05/xx-xy-book-marie-boulanger-gender-typography/)*

> “By association, letters become signs instantly perceived as male or female. This shifts focus away from the formal qualities of typefaces.”

Type designer [Marie Boulanger](http://marie-boulanger.com) has written a book titled [*XX, XY: Sex, Letters and Stereotypes*](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/08/05/xx-xy-book-marie-boulanger-gender-typography/) about how we make gender-based judgments through letterforms—a hotly debated topic in France—and how this perpetuates various gender stereotypes. Boulanger’s book was also mentioned in [an article by Angela Riechers](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/do-gender-neutral-pronouns-need-their-own-glyphs/) discussing the need for gender-neutral pronouns to have their own dedicated glyphs. 🇬🇧

## The mystery of the lowercase letter g

*Image/Video: [Video Source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSNGaJ0KiEs)*

> “We think if we look at something, especially if we have to pay attention to its shape as in reading, we know what it looks like, but the results show that is not always the case.” [Michael McCloskey](http://cogsci.jhu.edu/directory/michael-mccloskey/)

Johns Hopkins researchers conducted a [three-part experiment](https://hub.jhu.edu/2018/04/03/two-versions-of-letter-g-brain-science/) to test people’s awareness of the *g* they tend to write versus the *g* they tend to read. The experiment revealed that despite seeing it millions of times in almost every book, novel, newspaper, and email, people are actually unaware of the more common form of the lowercase letter *g*. 🇬🇧

## “What a stone carver can teach you about digital typography”

*Image: © [Amber Bravo](https://medium.com/google-design/typeset-in-stone-what-a-stone-carver-can-teach-you-about-digital-typography-aff8b7b69e1f)*

> “It’s not the computer that’s going to give me the ideal product, it’s my input into the machine that’s going to give the product.” Nick Benson

Google Design editor [Amber Bravo](https://twitter.com/amberbravo) conducted an in-depth [interview](https://medium.com/google-design/typeset-in-stone-what-a-stone-carver-can-teach-you-about-digital-typography-aff8b7b69e1f) with third-generation stone carver [Nick Benson](https://instagram.com/nixbenson) about the millennial history of modern stone carving and what digital type designers can learn from this ancient craft. 🇬🇧 Furthermore, in his [presentation](https://youtu.be/KSzZcop9qmA) at the [Span NYC](https://twitter.com/span_nyc) event in 2015, Nick Benson discussed what it means to practice an ancient form of typography in the digital age. 🇬🇧
