## A new way to bring the type world together: Typotheque Club

Netherlands-based type foundry [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/) announced [Typotheque Club](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/announcing-typotheque-club) to bring type users and type designers closer together. Free to join, Typotheque Club gives members access to previews of works in progress and free font trials, as well as rewards for purchases and invitations to exclusive online presentations offering insights into Typotheque’s research and design methodologies. 🇬🇧

## Modern handwriting: A historical survey

[In the bulletin we published in August](https://medium.com/tipografi/tipografi-b%C3%BClteni-a%C4%9Fustos-2023-5ee8b04702ec)—or haven’t you subscribed yet? :)—we mentioned TypeTogether’s comprehensive research on handwriting models taught in Western schools and their resulting project called [Primarium](https://primarium.info/). It appears that Typotheque is also conducting its own research. As part of this research, French researcher and author Sébastien Morlighem published a five-part [article series](https://www.typotheque.com/articles/modern-handwriting-a-historical-survey-1) on handwriting. 🇬🇧 In the series, Morlighem presents a historical survey of modern handwriting through articles covering new approaches in Great Britain, models from America and Western Europe, handwriting in Cyrillic and Greek scripts, and cursive italic handwriting in Central Europe. All this research was certainly not in vain! After researching educational handwriting models for over a year and documenting the evolution of handwriting in the Western world, the Typotheque team developed a typeface called [Dash](https://www.typotheque.com/blog/dash-a-typeface-with-different-speeds-and-expressions), which not only utilizes the collective characteristics of handwriting as taught in schools, but also mimics how handwriting transforms over time into an individual’s personal expression.

## Schriftenkartei: A Typeface Index

Michael Wörgötter [published](https://www.flickr.com/photos/letterformarchive/albums/72177720310834741) the complete contents of Schriftenkartei, showcasing around 620 metal typefaces from Germany’s last type foundries. Produced between 1958 and 1971, Schriftenkartei represents an agency’s effort in West Germany to catalog all typefaces being produced in the country at that time; because the specimens share a common format and fully display character sets, it is of great importance to type researchers and designers. *☞ [https://letterformarchive.org/news/schriftenkartei-german-font-index/](https://letterformarchive.org/news/schriftenkartei-german-font-index/)*Tap for more information. 🇬🇧

Type Designer [Taner Ardalı](https://www.taf-studio.com/) announced the typeface [SHB Grotesk](https://www.taf-studio.com/custom-fonts/shb-grotesk/), designed for Sahibinden.com, one of Turkey’s largest e-commerce and classified advertising platforms with 61.7 million users and 14.6 billion monthly page views.

Initiated in 2015 by the [Typofonderie Type Foundry](https://typofonderie.com/), the six-week type design program [TypeParis](https://typeparis.com/) will take place between June 4 and July 12, 2024. ## From mosaic name tablets to type design: Name Sans

Stephen Nixon, founder of [ArrowType](https://www.arrowtype.com/), on the [design process](https://www.arrowtype.com/articles/name-sans-process) of [Name Sans](https://www.arrowtype.com/name-sans)—a modern interpretation of the mosaic name tablets in New York subway stations, of which he recently released the first version. 🇬🇧

## A new typeface from Anna Khorash: Invert

The first version of [Invert](https://www.futurefonts.xyz/anna-khorash/invert), described by [Anna Khorash](https://www.instagram.com/horashann/) as “an experimental variable typeface with interpolation between shapes and counter-shapes,” has been released on Future Fonts. Invert features two diametrically opposed styles: two static styles—a black style based on a stencil structure and a white style formed by its counterforms—and one variable font. While the styles can work separately, the magic happens in the movement between them.

## A journey of discovery among endangered alphabets

Started eleven years ago by [Tim Brookes](https://twitter.com/TBAlphabets) as a Kickstarter campaign to help preserve and revitalize traditional writing systems, the [Endangered Alphabets](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/endangeredatlas/writing-beyond-writing) project has turned into a book thanks to donations from over three hundred and fifty people and support from experts and volunteers around the world. The book, titled [Writing Beyond Writing](https://www.endangeredalphabets.com/), traces endangered minority and indigenous scripts. 🇬🇧 ☞ *[https://youtu.be/JUkEUY8mVUE?si=OQaSKfwdQ-gU0uNJ](https://youtu.be/JUkEUY8mVUE?si=OQaSKfwdQ-gU0uNJ)* Interview with Tim Brookes

## A guide to learning Fraktur

Designed to teach Fraktur, one of the forms of blackletter, “[Blackletter — Fraktur Book](https://shop.itemzero.com/products/fraktur-book)” has been published, featuring over six hundred and eighty illustrations ranging from practice sheets to contemporary applications. 🇬🇧

## “The Typography Idea Book” published.

Steven Heller and Gail Anderson’s “[The Typography Idea Book](https://www.ketebe.com/tipografi-fikirler-kitabi-steven-heller-gail-anderson),” translated into Turkish, has been published by Ketebe. As part of a [four-book series](https://www.ketebe.com/tasarim-fikirler-kitap-seti-dort-kitap-steven-heller-gail-anderson) in the fields of graphic design, typography, logo, and illustration, it contains forty inspiring approaches developed by masters in the field from around the world.

## Inter updated.

Having a wide range of use from interface design to signage, covering 147 languages, and containing over two thousand glyphs, [Inter](https://rsms.me/inter/) has been updated. After working on the new version for two years, Type Designer [Rasmus Andersson](https://rsms.me/) [announced](https://twitter.com/rsms/status/1726394791427223564) that Inter has been updated with the addition of a Display face.

## Dr. Nilsu Atılgan at The Readability Consortium!

A non-profit organization made up of academic experts working on digital readability, [The Readability Consortium](https://thereadabilityconsortium.org/) [announced](https://twitter.com/TheReadabilityC/status/1727382886880366727) that [Dr. Nilsu Atılgan](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=i5pi1soAAAAJ&hl=en) has joined its research team. Having completed her PhD in cognitive and brain sciences at the University of Minnesota and postdoctoral research at Carnegie Mellon University, Atılgan was introduced as the team lead in the fields of *the psychophysics of variable typography and information design*.
